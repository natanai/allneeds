# “Needs are Gift” — Concept Notes

Source: S001 in `source-register.md`

Status: **mixed-authorship historical working material; none of the propositions below is accepted merely by appearing here**

These notes preserve the document's main conceptual claims and tensions without silently treating agent-written passages as Nat's settled position.

## Candidate NVC-derived axioms or theses present in the document

1. All human behavior is motivated by one or more underlying Needs.
2. Needs are universal.
3. Needs are ever-present or persistent rather than episodic in the manner of feelings.
4. Needs contain no reference to a specific person performing a specific action.
5. A Need can be met or unmet.
6. Feelings communicate or provide information about the present condition of Needs.
7. Needs are gifts, including when they are unmet.
8. Needs themselves should not be traded against other Needs.
9. Requests preserve the recipient's meaningful capacity to say no; demands do not.
10. A “no” can be understood as evidence that some concern remains unrepresented.
11. People are not responsible for producing or managing another person's feelings.
12. Compassion does not require sacrificing one person's Needs for another's.
13. Judgments may be “tragic expressions” of unmet Needs.
14. Strategies can be evaluated by whether they serve life and whether they sacrifice or obscure other Needs.
15. Nonviolence is presented as a third way between passivity and mirroring violence.
16. Ethical life may be grounded in recognizing Needs, preserving dignity, and collaboratively seeking strategies that do not coerce.

## Candidate construct distinctions

- **Need / feeling:** Needs are described as persistent; feelings as changing signals or language concerning Needs.
- **Need / strategy:** Needs are general and person/action-independent; strategies specify possible means.
- **Request / demand:** The distinction turns on whether refusal is genuinely permitted and whether coercive consequences attach.
- **Deserving / occurring:** A condition may deserve realization without actually being realized.
- **Care / responsibility:** Caring about another's experience is distinguished from responsibility for managing it.
- **Gift / transaction:** A gift is proposed to exclude demand, duty, or repayment owed to the giver.

## Ethical architecture suggested by the draft

The document does more than offer communication advice. It tentatively connects:

- motivation: Needs as sources of action;
- affect: feelings as information about Needs;
- ethics: non-coercive inclusion of all relevant Needs;
- interpersonal practice: observation, inquiry, requests, and hearing the Need behind “no”;
- spirituality: Christian nonviolence, dignity, gift, service to life, and “the kingdom of heaven”;
- flourishing: tending Needs without shame and with creative strategy formation.

## Important unresolved problems

### Universality

The draft sometimes treats a single counterexample as sufficient to disqualify a Need. That requires a much more precise account of population, development, impairment, measurement error, conceptual absence, atypical functioning, and culturally variable realization.

### Ever-presence

A distinction is needed between a disposition that persists, a currently active motive, an occurrent conscious desire, and an environmental condition. A Need might be dispositional without being continuously salient.

### “All behavior”

The universal claim must distinguish purposive action from reflex, tic, seizure, automaticity, habit, compulsion, and behavior whose causal contribution is distributed across multiple systems.

### Feelings as language

The metaphor may support compassionate inquiry, but it currently risks implying a decoder relation. The theory must specify whether feelings are caused by Need satisfaction, track appraisals relevant to Needs, merely correlate with concern states, or are transformed by conceptual and social interpretation.

### No responsibility for feelings

Mediation does not erase interpersonal causation or moral responsibility. The theory must distinguish causal contribution, responsibility, blame, obligation, control, and emotion-management labor.

### Needs do not trade

The draft's moral refusal to invalidate one concern is distinct from the empirical possibility of scarce resources, incompatible actions, and tragic trade-offs. Legitimacy, compatibility, and joint satisfiability require separate propositions.

### “Needs are gifts”

At least four readings must be separated:

- ontological: having Needs is intrinsically good;
- epistemic: Needs or their affective manifestations disclose what matters;
- motivational: Needs make care and action possible;
- practical/spiritual: relating to Needs as gifts reduces shame or violence.

Evidence for one reading would not establish the others.

### Requests and demands

The distinction cannot rest on sentence form alone. It likely requires operational markers involving credible freedom to refuse, threatened consequences, power, dependency, retaliation, negotiation, and the requester's response to refusal.

### “Serve life”

The phrase requires a declared standard of functioning. Biological viability, agency, integration, relationship, morality, and flourishing are not interchangeable.
