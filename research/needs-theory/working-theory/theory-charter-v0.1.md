# Theory Charter v0.1

Status: provisional agent synthesis for Nat's review; not Nat-authored doctrine and not production content.

## Research identity

The project is a critical theoretical reconstruction of NVC. Loyalty attaches to possible phenomena that NVC noticed, not to preserving Rosenberg's explanations unchanged.

## Target domain

Explain how recurring person-environment concerns become salient, influence appraisal and affect, become represented as values and goals, bias action selection, and become communicated or inferred in interpersonal interaction.

Initial unit of analysis:

[
\text{person} \times \text{environment} \times \text{time}
]

## Claim-status grammar

- **DEF:** stipulated technical definition
- **OBS:** particular report or recorded occurrence
- **PHEN:** stable empirical generalization requiring explanation
- **HYP:** falsifiable proposed relationship
- **POST:** provisional explanatory principle
- **MEAS:** inference from an operation or instrument to a construct
- **INT:** intervention claim
- **NORM:** normative commitment

A claim must not silently change type.

## Provisional construct grammar

- **Concern:** a dispositional sensitivity within a person-environment system to a class of conditions, capable of altering attention, appraisal, affect, valuation, or action tendency.
- **Need:** a concern whose sustained deprivation or frustration reliably produces characteristic impairment relative to a specified domain of functioning, population, and timescale, beyond loss of one preferred strategy.
- **Basic Need:** a Need occupying a minimally replaceable explanatory role in the best-supported theory of human functioning.
- **Value:** a relatively trans-situational, symbolically represented priority concerning what is important, desirable, right, or worth pursuing.
- **Motive:** a concern in a state of activation that biases attention, appraisal, valuation, persistence, or action selection at a particular time.
- **Goal:** a represented target state.
- **Strategy:** a possible means or policy for approaching a goal.
- **Feeling:** a phenomenological state arising from bodily, appraisal, conceptual, historical, and contextual processes.

## Provisional postulates

1. **Situated determination:** behavior is jointly produced by concern activation, appraisal, learning, bodily state, affordances, habits, social context, and material constraints.
2. **Concern-relative salience:** external conditions become motivationally salient through interaction with concern organization.
3. **Motivational activation:** a concern becomes a motive when actual, anticipated, remembered, or imagined conditions make it action-relevant.
4. **Many-to-many organization:** one concern may be pursued through several strategies, and one strategy may serve several concerns.
5. **Competitive and constrained action selection:** concerns bias possible actions rather than mechanically producing one response.
6. **Affective underdetermination:** feelings provide fallible evidence about concern-relevant conditions but do not uniquely identify a Need.
7. **Representational elaboration:** development and culture can reorganize motivation through values, identities, beliefs, and goals.
8. **Basicness through characteristic impairment:** basic status requires characteristic deprivation effects plus explanatory value beyond adjacent constructs.
9. **Articulation may transform:** naming a concern can change attention, self-understanding, goal representation, social inference, and strategy generation.
10. **Ontology and morality are independent:** causal reality, functional necessity, moral defensibility, permissible strategy, and another person's obligation do not entail one another.
11. **Need non-conflict requires decomposition:** equal legitimacy, logical compatibility, and practical joint satisfiability are separate claims.
12. **NVC is multi-component:** observation, affect labeling, concern identification, strategy separation, empathic attention, and request formation may have different mechanisms.

## Minimum criteria for basicness

A candidate Basic Need must earn:

1. functional consequences under sustained deprivation or frustration;
2. cross-context recurrence;
3. multiple realizability through materially different strategies;
4. discriminant validity from neighboring constructs;
5. incremental explanatory value;
6. mechanistic plausibility;
7. developmental evidence;
8. cross-cultural generality;
9. directional or causal evidence; and
10. explicit disconfirmation conditions.

## Initial risky predictions

- Blocking one strategy while providing a materially different effective strategy should preserve some benefit if the construct is genuinely strategy-independent.
- Chronic frustration across contexts and strategies should produce a recognizable impairment signature if a candidate is basic.
- A distinct Basic Need should predict something beyond general distress, adjacent constructs, personality, social desirability, and shared method variance.
- The same feeling should occur under multiple concern configurations, and the same concern with multiple feelings.
- A universal Need should retain a similar functional role across cultures despite different labels, thresholds, and strategies.
- Need, value, and motive should sometimes dissociate.
- Concern-strategy separation should increase strategy diversity beyond suitable active controls if it is an active NVC mechanism.
- NVC practices may improve outcomes even if NVC's inherited ontology is partly false.

## Immediate foundational review

The next foundational question is:

> What can “basic human Need” scientifically mean?

The review should compare philosophical necessity, organismic regulation, psychological nutriment, evolved motive systems, cybernetic reference conditions, and value-relative functioning before any individual candidate term is adjudicated.
