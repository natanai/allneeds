# Observation evidence audit — Round 3 (direct NVC remaining studies, overlooked syntheses and 2026 trial)

**Date:** 2026-10-12. **Repository:** natanai/allneeds, site-test **documentation only**. All existing and newly discovered research sources remain **PENDING USER APPROVAL**. No published content updated or deploy authorized.

## Research approach and limitations

This is purposive research with original publisher/DOI/PubMed/open university source checks, not a PRISMA systematic review. For N11 and N15, full accessible articles and trial details were checked, including denominators, comparators and outcomes. For N07–N10 and N12 full-study access remains limited; the source descriptions below distinguish publisher abstracts from full-text analyses. Primary sources, not NVC advocacy websites, anchor research findings. Source URLs are direct canonical DOI links with no ChatGPT or tracking parameters.

Causal inference distinctions: single-arm before/after ≠ controlled trial; uncontrolled role-play ratings ≠ blinded real-world patient outcomes; subjective questionnaire ≠ objectively observed interpersonal skill; randomized allocation alone ≠ unbiased study if allocation concealment, outcome masking, primary endpoint prespecification or equivalent intervention dose is uncertain.

## N07 — Museux, Dumont, Careau & Milot (2016), Improving interprofessional collaboration: The effect of training in nonviolent communication

**Canonical source:** https://doi.org/10.1080/00981389.2016.1164270

**Verified access:** Publisher full landing page and PubMed abstract; downloadable copy claimed on ResearchGate but not inspected as manuscript

**Design / outcomes extracted:** Two teams (N=9), mixed methods before/after training; observation instruments Team Observed Structured Clinical Encounter and Observed Interprofessional Collaboration, plus focus groups; modest gains described for role clarification, family-centered collaboration and shared planning.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** Feasibility and observed interprofessional team skills only; N=9, no meaningful causal comparison or blinding information checked, and team/group measures not independent patient clinical outcomes.

**Editorial recommendation:** Low certainty, supporting contextual citation only. REQUEST FULL TEXT if reviewer wants table-level assessment.

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** An exploratory two-team study reported improvements on structured interprofessional collaboration assessments after NVC training; the small uncontrolled sample prevents strong causal conclusions.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## N08 — Kim & Kim (2022), NVC-based anger management in psychiatric inpatients

**Canonical source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apnu.2022.07.004

**Verified access:** Publisher and PubMed abstracts only; full original paper behind access barrier

**Design / outcomes extracted:** Six 60-minute one-to-one sessions, nonequivalent control pre/post comparison. Significant changes in anger expression and suppression, nonsignificant self-esteem and aggression outcomes. Reported total 44, but intervention n=21 and control n=24 sum to 45.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** Resolve discrepancy in participant denominators (44 vs 21+24=45) and study attrition/analysis sample before any numerical claim. Self-report anger endpoints do not establish reduced aggressive acts; nonrandomized, no matched treatment comparator.

**Editorial recommendation:** Hold detailed efficacy assertions. FULL TEXT REQUESTED only if user can obtain an authorized free copy.

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** In a small quasi-experimental inpatient study, NVC-based anger education was associated with improvements on certain anger-expression measures, but not statistically clear improvements in aggression or self-esteem.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## N09 — Yang & Kim (2021), NVC communication training in hospitalized people with alcohol-related disorders

**Canonical source:** https://doi.org/10.1111/ppc.12673

**Verified access:** Publisher abstract and references; Wiley 'full' resolves to abstract in available interface

**Design / outcomes extracted:** N=47 patients recruited in wards of two hospitals. Mixed-method quasi-experiment. Abstract reports p<.001 improvement in measured empathy, anger expression, communication competence and abstinence self-efficacy.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** Alcohol abstinence self-efficacy is NOT measured abstinence or reduced relapse. Need allocation mechanism, comparator, outcome timepoints, exact sample per group, measures, dropout, and between-group effect sizes from full text. Do not use authors' suggested relapse-prevention clinical implication as established fact.

**Editorial recommendation:** Hold specific effects until full methods. FULL TEXT REQUESTED only if legitimately available at no cost.

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** A small inpatient quasi-experimental study reported improvements in self-reported communication-related outcomes after NVC-based training, but did not establish actual relapse prevention.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## N10 — Nosek & Durán (2017), Increasing empathy and conflict-resolution skills through NVC training in Latino adults and youth

**Canonical source:** https://doi.org/10.1353/cpr.2017.0032

**Verified access:** University of San Francisco author-deposited accepted manuscript listing and PubMed abstract; manuscript file downloadable at https://repository.usfca.edu/nursing_fac/120/ but not yet studied line-by-line

**Design / outcomes extracted:** Four culturally and linguistically adapted community sessions led by bilingual trainers. Adult/youth pre/post focus groups describe changed ways to recognize and manage conflict. Attendance variability explicitly precluded quantitative analyses.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** Focus groups document perceived experience and acceptability, not independently measured conflict reduction; without quantitative comparisons or equivalent control no efficacy claim. No universal cultural extrapolation.

**Editorial recommendation:** Retain for community implementation/cultural adaptation, NOT intervention efficacy.

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** An NVC community-training project reported qualitative participant reflections about empathy and conflict handling, but could not quantify changes because attendance was inconsistent.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## N11 — Sung & Kweon (2022), Effects of an NVC-based empathy education program in nursing students

**Canonical source:** https://doi.org/10.3390/nursrep12040080

**Verified access:** Full publisher article and tables (MDPI/PMC), all principal methods/results accessible

**Design / outcomes extracted:** Pilot with 62 volunteers from two nursing programs, n=32 training/n=30 comparison, authors classify as quasi-experimental but explicitly say assignment by coin toss. No dropouts. Program 6x120 minute sessions (12 hours) with lectures, role-play, group discussion, feelings/needs cards, feedback and journal reflections. Comparator NVC principles/feelings/needs mini cards (not an active training program). One week after course, mean self-report pre/post change in empathy +22.28 (SD17.72) versus -1.13 (SD17.59), difference 23.41, t=5.22, p<.001. Other self-report outcomes self-esteem +4.56 vs -0.07; interpersonal relationships +14.59 vs -3.00; communication competence +9.53 vs -0.87, each p<.001.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** Important not to call a coin-toss assignment automatically a high-quality registered RCT; independent allocation concealment/assessor masking not reported, authors themselves call it quasi-experimental. All outcomes are questionnaires within one week; possible expectation/demand effect; no longer-term outcomes, clinical patient interactions or component-isolation. Small two-school Korean convenience sample, no registration identified.

**Editorial recommendation:** Promising SHORT-TERM self-report experimental signal. Retain with caveats, request no user file (fully accessible).

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** In a 62-person nursing-education pilot, a 12-hour NVC-based course yielded greater short-term gains in several self-report measures than access to NVC reference cards. Effects on actual patient communication and durability were not assessed.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## N12 — Zandkarimi, Kamelifar & Heshmati-Molaee (2019), Nonviolence communication to reduce stress, anxiety and depression in young Iranian women

**Original publisher article URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10560-018-0584-y

**DOI (metadata only):** 10.1007/s10560-018-0584-y

**Verified access:** Full original publisher landing page and abstract, including intervention, eligibility, instrument, follow-up and outcome-specific conclusion. Detailed tables and full paper remain subscription-restricted.

**Design / outcomes extracted:** Fifty girls aged 11–18 from charity centers around Tehran were randomly allocated equally (25/25) to eight NVC group training sessions or a no-intervention control. Participants completed DASS-21 pre-/post-assessments and a one-month follow-up. The Springer abstract reports a between-group difference (p<.001) and explicitly states **stress and anxiety decreased, but depression did not improve**. Exact domain-specific effect sizes, interval estimates, allocation concealment, and missing-data handling are not visible in the abstract.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** The title's 'young women' population actually includes children/adolescents, not adults. No-treatment control may not control for group attention, social support or expectation. The null depression finding must never be hidden behind generic 'mental health improved' claims. Need full-text table check before interpreting outcome-specific p-values, attrition and bias.

**Editorial recommendation:** Relevant randomized adolescent study; retain in research queue, **hold effectiveness claim** until full-text assessment.

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** 'A small randomized study among economically vulnerable Iranian adolescent girls reported lower stress and anxiety after eight NVC group sessions compared with a no-intervention group, but not improved depression; precise effects and study quality require full-text review.'

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## N13 — Yang & Kim (2016), Integrative review of NVC intervention studies

**Canonical source:** https://doi.org/10.16952/pns.2016.13.1.36

**Verified access:** Original indexed peer-reviewed review/English abstract, bilingual Korean full-text versions available via publisher and scholarly repositories

**Design / outcomes extracted:** Searched five Korean academic databases for 2005-2015; 12 eligible studies: 9 quasi-experimental (75%), plus one qualitative, one mixed-method and one case study. Most tested NVC group programs, often about 10-11 sessions. Authors conclude stronger-designed clinical research needed.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** Not comparable directly with 2024 scoping review N01, whose different period, publication languages and healthcare-specific eligibility produce seven studies. Korean full text needs dedicated extraction and language-aware appraisal to assess included-study overlap/bias.

**Editorial recommendation:** Very high priority evidence-mapping source; new candidate, all approval pending.

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** A Korean-database integrative review identified twelve earlier NVC intervention studies, mostly quasi-experimental, and recommended more rigorous trials.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## N14 — Nosek, Gifford & Kober (2014), NVC training increases empathy in baccalaureate nursing students: mixed methods

**Canonical source:** https://doi.org/10.5430/jnep.v4n10p1

**Verified access:** Open journal and University of San Francisco manuscript; publication abstract verified, full study PDF available for next tables pass

**Design / outcomes extracted:** Single-group pre/post design with self-rated Interpersonal Reactivity Index; mean score 69.1 before to 71.4 after, p=.037; reflective journals and focus groups were qualitative components, including participants followed up qualitatively at two years.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** No untreated comparison: regression to mean, classroom maturation, selection and demand characteristics uncontrolled; numerical change small. Two-year qualitative feedback is not two-year quantitatively measured empathy retention.

**Editorial recommendation:** Retain only as descriptive preliminary NVC education study; new pending candidate.

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** A single-group nursing-student study found a small increase on a self-reported empathy questionnaire after NVC education; without a control group it cannot identify the training as its cause.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## N15 — Jiang, Yuan, Wang, Qiu & Bian (2026), NVC training among medical students: randomized educational trial

**Canonical source:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09621-w

**Verified access:** Full 26-page accepted manuscript/article-in-press PDF from publisher (the manuscript is not yet a copy-edited final version); supplementary tables linked but not yet independently checked

**Design / outcomes extracted:** Single-center fourth-year medical students in China; 314 invited, 198 consented, randomized NVC n=100 vs time-matched standard communication instruction n=98. All 198 did baseline and immediate post-course. Five 2-hour sessions over one month. Full 20-item JSPE primary self-report outcome group-by-time difference +16.95 (95% CI 15.40-18.50, p<.001), Hedges g=3.02; a newly condensed eight-item Calgary-Cambridge-derived OSCE-style checklist, group-by-time difference +4.44 (95% CI 3.41-5.47, p<.001), g=1.19, with blinded group raters but NOT blinded to assessment time. Average-measure rater ICC 0.891 baseline, 0.946 post. Not prospectively registered; authors had no formal a priori sample size calculation; no long-term follow-up.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** A strikingly large self-reported empathy change warrants independent replication and raw instrument/analysis review. Control had contact time but NOT comparable deliberate-practice and corrective-feedback exercises, so causal improvement cannot be attributed specifically to NVC concepts over practice intensity. OSCE simulated, eight-item instrument adapted for this study rather than a fully psychometrically validated clinical competency test. Study did not isolate any one NVC component, nor measure real patient outcomes. The final publisher version and supplement should be rechecked before approval.

**Editorial recommendation:** Strongest new candidate for short-term observed role-play training benefit; important design risks mean HOLD public causal language until supplement and replication evaluation.

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** One single-center active-controlled educational trial reported larger short-term gains in self-rated empathy and simulated communication ratings after an NVC-based practice curriculum, but nonidentical practice content, study-specific outcome checklist and lack of preregistration limit interpretation.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## M02 — Fiedler, Semin & Bolten (1989), Language use and reification of social information

**Canonical source:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.2420190403

**Verified access:** Original full publisher abstract; author institution records; full article accessible through Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam repository link

**Design / outcomes extracted:** Three social-cognition experiments: serial retelling of person descriptions (abstraction increases in communication), priming prior abstract descriptors before judgments of behaviors, and challenging truth of generalizations (shift back toward more concrete language). Supports studying linguistic abstraction and ensuing judgments, not just verb/adjective preference.

**Risk of bias / applicability:** Context/task-specific social cognition. Abstract phrasing may shape inferences, but cannot establish that every abstract descriptor is inaccurate or that details prevent biased recollection; older literature needs contemporary replication/critique. Academic claim should not imply intentions/experiences are unimportant.

**Editorial recommendation:** Retain as original fundamental linguistic abstraction research, not proof of NVC observational therapeutic efficacy.

**Candidate public claim (NOT APPROVED):** In experimental social-judgment settings, more abstract descriptions of people could influence how later information was interpreted; this does not determine the truth of any particular interpersonal account.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **User claim decision:** NOT PRESENTED FOR PUBLICATION.

## Ongoing critical risks and review plan

- The 2016 Korean integrative review and the 2024 healthcare scoping review are not pooled, because their inclusion rules, population and dates differ; identify duplicate primary trials after extracting the 2016 review's full references.
- The 2026 Jiang trial reports unusually large effects (self-rated empathy g=3.02), so do not accept the strength of causal attribution without checking supplementary material, outcome reliability, clustering, contamination, selective reporting and replication. Randomization and 100% completion are strengths; absence of preregistration and structurally different practice doses are limits.
- Several apparent NVC outcomes rely heavily on self-rated empathy and communication. The Observation → Feeling → Need → Strategy pathway is conceptual and a design choice, NOT validated as a precise mechanism by any source screened here.
- The older Fiedler experimental line of research on the social impact of abstract descriptions should be incorporated as a separate cognitive-science topic, with careful safeguards against implying that the teller's experience is untrue.
