# Understanding Content Audit

> Status: fully audited and live as of 2026-08-26. The approved content package, function-lens presentation, strategy deck, and Understanding · Converging Map (U4D) production magnet are all deployed.

## Approved page structure

Understanding is the first approved Need to use the optional function-lens model defined in `docs/need-function-lenses.md`.

The page keeps one canonical Need and one umbrella description, then presents two simultaneously visible functions:

1. **Making sense**: `I want to understand this.`
2. **Understanding between people**: `I want us to understand each other.`

These are reader-facing recognition lenses, not competing scientific definitions or separate Needs. Each lens owns the evidence that supports its narrower claims. There is no additional umbrella citation list merely for symmetry.

## Approved umbrella copy

> Humans and other animals actively seek information when uncertainty matters, and human cooperation often involves coordinating attention, intentions, and meanings with other people. This need may draw us to ask questions, seek explanations, check assumptions, make our own thinking easier to grasp, and listen for what we may have missed. Understanding does not require certainty or agreement. Sometimes tending it means building a clearer picture of what is happening; sometimes it means finding where two people's meanings align or differ.

## Lens 1: Making sense

**Recognition cue:** `I want to understand this.`

Approved short lens copy:

> Trying to make an event, idea, pattern, cause, or uncertainty more intelligible. This can draw us to notice what we do not yet know, gather information, ask questions, compare explanations, and revise our picture as new information becomes available.

Approved expanded copy:

> Uncertainty creates a practical information problem. Humans and other animals often seek information that can improve present decisions, and they sometimes seek information even when it has no immediate instrumental payoff. Ajuwon and colleagues' comparative review places these behaviors in a broader animal context while emphasizing that the evolutionary origins of curiosity remain unresolved. This supports treating information-seeking as a real and consequential behavioral tendency without claiming that every desire to understand is an evolved adaptation with one specific origin. [1]
>
> Information-seeking is also more varied than simply collecting as much information as possible. Gottlieb and colleagues review mechanisms ranging from responses to novelty and surprise to more systematic searches for learning progress and information gain. Gottlieb and Oudeyer later distinguish information sampling, where an organism reduces uncertainty relevant to a current task, from more open-ended information search. These distinctions help explain why Understanding can sometimes feel like finding one missing answer and at other times like exploring a topic until a more coherent picture develops. [2][3]
>
> Curiosity is closely related to this process but should not be treated as identical to Understanding. Kidd and Hayden describe curiosity as an important motivator of learning and information-seeking while also noting that the field lacks a single accepted definition or comprehensive account of its mechanisms and functions. Understanding is therefore the broader everyday Need label used here. The research supports motivations to seek, organize, and use information, not a scientific claim that all of those processes form one formally established psychological need. [4]
>
> Tending this side of Understanding can therefore mean identifying the part that remains unclear, seeking information that could reduce that uncertainty, explaining the current model to ourselves, and staying willing to revise it. Reaching a useful understanding does not require eliminating every uncertainty. Sometimes the most informative outcome is discovering exactly what is still unknown.

Approved sources:

1. Ajuwon, V., Monteiro, T., Schnell, A. K., & Clayton, N. S. (2025). _To know or not to know? Curiosity and the value of prospective information in animals._ https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13420-024-00647-y
2. Gottlieb, J., Oudeyer, P.-Y., Lopes, M., & Baranes, A. (2013). _Information-seeking, curiosity, and attention: computational and neural mechanisms._ https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4193662/
3. Gottlieb, J., & Oudeyer, P.-Y. (2018). _Towards a neuroscience of active sampling and curiosity._ https://www.nature.com/articles/s41583-018-0078-0
4. Kidd, C., & Hayden, B. Y. (2015). _The Psychology and Neuroscience of Curiosity._ https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4635443/

Internal source-role notes:

- **[1] Ajuwon et al.:** comparative and evolutionary context for prospective information-seeking. The review explicitly leaves the evolutionary origins of curiosity unresolved. It does not establish an adaptation named Understanding.
- **[2] Gottlieb et al. 2013:** broad theoretical/computational review of information-seeking, attention, novelty, surprise, uncertainty, and learning progress. Useful mechanism synthesis, not evidence for a discrete psychological Need.
- **[3] Gottlieb & Oudeyer 2018:** supports distinctions among active sampling and more open-ended curiosity/information search. The neuroscience framing should not be used to imply that one user-facing exercise is neurologically proven.
- **[4] Kidd & Hayden:** broad review establishing curiosity as an important motivator of learning and information-seeking while emphasizing definitional and theoretical uncertainty in the field.

## Lens 2: Understanding between people

**Recognition cue:** `I want us to understand each other.`

Approved short lens copy:

> Trying to grasp another person's meaning and help them grasp ours well enough to communicate or coordinate. This can involve listening, explaining, checking interpretations, noticing different perspectives, and finding where meanings match or diverge.

Approved expanded copy:

> Human cooperation provides one context for why interpersonal understanding matters. Tomasello and colleagues propose that human cognition is distinctive partly because people participate in collaborative activities involving shared goals, attention, and intentions. Their Shared Intentionality Theory offers an influential evolutionary account of how coordinating psychological states may have contributed to human cooperation and cultural cognition. It should remain explicitly labeled as a theory. A later comparative and developmental review by Sauciuc and Persson argues that evidence challenges some of its stronger claims, particularly the proposed late evolutionary origin of shared intentionality and a sharp divide between human and ape psychology. [1][2]
>
> Coordination does not require everyone to hold an identical picture. DeChurch and Mesmer-Magnus synthesized 23 independent studies of shared team mental models. Shared mental models were positively related to team performance, but the observed relationships depended in meaningful ways on how shared knowledge was represented and measured. This supports the practical value of sufficiently compatible understandings for coordination without establishing that agreement or cognitive uniformity is always desirable. [3]
>
> Relationships add another important distinction. Actually understanding another person, believing that we understand them, and feeling understood by them are not the same thing. Reis, Lemay, and Finkenauer review research showing that people's perceptions of being understood are only modestly related to actually being understood. In a study of 152 couples discussing conflict, Hinnekens and colleagues likewise found that perceived understanding was positively associated with dyadic adjustment while measured empathic accuracy was not. The study was associational and does not show that simply making someone feel understood causes a better relationship. It does show why interpersonal Understanding cannot be reduced to one objective accuracy score. [4][5]
>
> Tending this side of Understanding can therefore involve more than deciding whether one person's interpretation is correct. We can ask whether we have grasped what another person means, whether they experience us as having understood them, whether our own meaning has been communicated clearly, and where our perspectives still differ. Understanding can improve while disagreement remains.

Approved sources:

1. Tomasello, M., Carpenter, M., Call, J., Behne, T., & Moll, H. (2005). _Understanding and sharing intentions: the origins of cultural cognition._ https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16262930/
2. Sauciuc, G.-A., & Persson, T. (2023). _Empirical challenges from the comparative and developmental literature to the Shared Intentionality Theory._ https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37901066/
3. DeChurch, L. A., & Mesmer-Magnus, J. R. (2010). _Measuring shared team mental models: A meta-analysis._ https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017455
4. Reis, H. T., Lemay, E. P., Jr., & Finkenauer, C. (2017). _Toward understanding understanding: The importance of feeling understood in relationships._ https://doi.org/10.1111/spc3.12308
5. Hinnekens, C., Stas, L., Gistelinck, F., & Verhofstadt, L. L. (2020). _“I think you understand me.” Studying the associations between actual, assumed, and perceived understanding within couples._ https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.2614

Internal source-role notes:

- **[1] Tomasello et al.:** influential Shared Intentionality Theory. Use only as an explicitly theoretical evolutionary account, not settled fact about human uniqueness or one exact selection history.
- **[2] Sauciuc & Persson:** deliberate counterweight documenting comparative and developmental challenges to stronger Shared Intentionality Theory claims.
- **[3] DeChurch & Mesmer-Magnus:** meta-analysis of 23 independent studies. Supports a relationship between shared mental models and team performance while showing that representation and measurement matter. It does not establish that uniform agreement is always optimal.
- **[4] Reis et al.:** integrative relationship review distinguishing actual understanding from perceived/experienced understanding.
- **[5] Hinnekens et al.:** couple study directly distinguishing actual, assumed, and perceived understanding. Its associations with dyadic adjustment are not causal evidence.

## Approved static Understanding strategy deck

### `turn-gap-into-question`

- **Title:** Turn the gap into a question
- **Need:** Understanding
- **Wording:** `Write one sentence about what you are trying to understand. Then finish: “The part I still do not understand is ___.” Turn that gap into one question you could investigate.`
- **Primary pathway:** Making sense
- **Evidence classification:** clinically grounded educational translation
- **Supporting source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543066002181
- **Public link label:** `Supporting source ↗`
- **Limitation:** Rosenshine, Meister, and Chapman review question-generation interventions in educational comprehension. The card translates the supported question-generation principle to a broader low-risk sense-making prompt; the exact card has not been trialed.

### `explain-in-your-own-words`

- **Title:** Explain it in your own words
- **Need:** Understanding
- **Wording:** `Choose the idea or situation you are trying to understand. Explain your current understanding in plain language without checking the source. Notice the first place your explanation becomes vague, then look back or gather information there.`
- **Primary pathway:** Making sense
- **Evidence classification:** direct intervention evidence plus low-risk translation
- **Supporting source:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x
- **Public link label:** `Supporting source ↗`
- **Limitation:** Bisra and colleagues meta-analyze induced self-explanation for learning. The step of noticing where one's explanation becomes vague is a restrained user-facing translation rather than a separately tested protocol.

### `reflect-and-check`

- **Title:** Reflect and check
- **Need:** Understanding
- **Wording:** `In a conversation, briefly say what you think the other person means, then ask whether you got it right. If not, let them correct the part you missed before you respond.`
- **Primary pathway:** Understanding between people
- **Evidence classification:** direct experimental evidence plus established active-listening practice
- **Supporting source:** https://doi.org/10.1080/10904018.2013.813234
- **Public link label:** `Supporting source ↗`
- **Limitation:** Weger and colleagues evaluated an active-listening response package, not this exact one-sentence card in isolation. The card does not claim that reflective paraphrasing guarantees accurate understanding or relationship improvement.

The deck intentionally has two Making sense strategies and one interpersonal strategy. No fourth card is added merely to create symmetry between lenses.

## Function-lens implementation scope

Approval authorizes the generic optional function-lens capability as part of this implementation:

- canonical lens data in `src/data/editorialCatalog.json`;
- deterministic propagation through the runtime catalog compiler and `Need` model;
- generic Need-page rendering with both lenses visibly present together;
- stacked narrow-screen presentation and responsive side-by-side presentation when space permits;
- a recognition cue, short lens copy, optional Details narrative, and lens-local citations;
- no tabs, no slug-specific Understanding rendering, and no runtime repair layer;
- no automatic retrofit of other Needs.

Strategies remain associated with the canonical Need, not with lens IDs.
