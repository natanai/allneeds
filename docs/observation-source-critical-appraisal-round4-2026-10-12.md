## Question and search method

**Question:** What evidence examines independent steps or interactions relevant to `Observation → Feeling → Need → Strategy`, as distinct from evidence for a longer NVC training program? The NVC framework remains an intentional design vocabulary, not an empirically validated clinical pathway.

**Search approach (targeted discovery, NOT a systematic literature review):** On 2026-10-12, search public academic article databases, journals and institutional repositories for "nonviolent communication component dismantling / factorial / four components", "affect labeling reappraisal naming effects", "emotion differentiation systematic review", "expressive writing / emotional disclosure meta-analysis", "SDT health intervention meta-analysis", "linguistic abstraction concrete criticism experiment", and "implementation intentions goal attainment meta-analysis"; follow publisher/DOI/PubMed and open article records, with contrary-result retrieval rather than only favorable studies. Record canonical DOI routes without ChatGPT redirects or tracking parameters. No reliable randomized dismantling trial that directly isolated each of NVC's four components was located in **these searches**; that is **not evidence none exists**. Further database-specific and citation-chaining search remains necessary.

**Access status:** Complete original article (where stated) or publisher/PubMed abstract/record. Do NOT conflate abstract review with systematic full-text bias review. Entries below are candidate-level. Publication dates and review styles differ; syntheses may reuse primary trials and MUST NOT be treated as independent counts.

## Component evidence summary

| AllNeeds step | What adjacent research directly tests | Major unresolved inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation | How specific/abstract wording changes social judgments and reactions to criticism. | Neither truth, intent, objectivity, clinical validity, nor NVC's complete Observation method established. |
| Feeling | Emotion naming alone and before reappraisal/acceptance; emotion differentiation associations. | Effects on subjective wellbeing can be positive, null or counterproductive in some tasks; no universally beneficial naming sequence. |
| Need | Programs that support autonomy, competence and relatedness in Self-Determination Theory. | Explicitly naming or inferring a particular NVC Need has not been isolated as an effective mechanism by these studies. |
| Strategy | Forming specific if-then action plans. | A suggested or chosen Strategy is not the same as performing a studied, planned action. |
| Journal/disclosure | Experimental expressive-disclosure protocols and meta-analyses. | Benefits generally small/inconsistent across outcomes and not direct evidence about AllNeeds journal or valence/arousal tracking. |
| Whole sequence | NVC workshops and education packages documented in N-series sources. | Effects can't be attributed to the four steps, their order or the AllNeeds interface absent suitable component comparisons. |

### C01 — Nook, Satpute & Ochsner (2021), Emotion Naming Impedes Both Cognitive Reappraisal and Mindful Acceptance Strategies of Emotion Regulation

**Original publisher/DOI URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s42761-021-00036-y

**Domain:** Feeling step / sequencing

**Evidence access:** Full original open publisher paper: both Methods and Results

**Design:** Study 1 randomized factorial between-groups N=80, four conditions (look, name, reappraise, name+reappraise); Study 2 within-participant N=60 after 72 consented (11 excluded for failing labeling instructions, 1 dropped). Negative standardized images, not real interpersonal events.

**Findings:** Emotion naming alone did not significantly lower self-reported unpleasant affect vs viewing; naming before reappraisal reduced improvement compared to reappraisal alone (Study 1: t(38)=-3.82, p<.001, d=-1.21). Study 2 extends to mindful acceptance and reduces some timing concerns.

**Limits and potentially disconfirming facts:** Laboratory negative images, narrow undergraduate samples, short-run affect ratings. Study 1 shared 12-s window between naming and regulating and had an image-timing coding error, acknowledged by authors; study 2 improved the control but excluded instruction-noncompliant participants. Cannot infer the NVC Feeling stage is unsafe or always counterproductive.

**Proposed source use (NOT approved site wording):** Important direct component-interaction and contradictory evidence; public language must say impact is context/sequence dependent, not universal.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **Public claim decision:** PENDING. **Integration:** prohibited until approval.

### C02 — Ariely, Mokady, Reggev & Anholt (2026), Affect Labeling and Reappraisal as an Emotion Regulation Strategy

**Original publisher/DOI URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s42761-026-00362-z

**Domain:** Feeling step / sequencing

**Evidence access:** Full original open Springer paper; abstract and core methods/results

**Design:** Two experiments pooled N=226, four image-viewing conditions: passive observation, name emotion, reappraise, name+reappraise. Same negative images, immediate measurement and follow-up 1–2 days later.

**Findings:** Replication of inhibitory effect: labeling before reappraisal reduced immediate self-rated affect improvement relative to reappraisal without labeling. Follow-up did not support predicted delayed labeling benefit; conditions returned toward baseline.

**Limits and potentially disconfirming facts:** Authors describe potential 'crystallization' mechanism as theory, not independently verified causal mediator. Image tasks and required word choices are not a voluntary feelings check-in or a therapy trial. Need independent extraction of each experiment and allocation/missing follow-up before confident magnitude.

**Proposed source use (NOT approved site wording):** Strong candidate to corroborate conditional role of emotion naming. No categorical 'always beneficial' or 'always harmful' claim.

**User source decision:** PENDING. **Public claim decision:** PENDING. **Integration:** prohibited until approval.

### C03 — Jang, Hwang & Shin (2026), A Systematic Review of the Effectiveness of Affect Labeling: Is Affect Labeling Effective for Emotion Regulation?

**Original publisher/DOI URL:** https://doi.org/10.17315/kjhp.2026.31.4.009

**Domain:** Feeling step / systematic review

**Evidence access:** Korea Citation Index peer-reviewed Korean-language journal record with complete English abstract; Korean full text not yet independently extracted

**Design:** PRISMA-described literature review using three Korean and four international academic databases; publications 2005–2024; 43 eligible affect-labeling studies.

**Findings:** Across studies, physiological/behavioral response patterns tended to show reduced or stabilized reactivity, whereas self-reported emotions had positive, null and adverse/increasing intensity effects. Moderators included labeling context and format and individual factors.

**Limits and potentially disconfirming facts:** Qualitative systematic synthesis, not confirmed pooled meta-analysis; risk-of-bias methodology and publication list require Korean full-text review, and review-level directional summaries cannot quantify clinical effect. Not a trial of AllNeeds or NVC.

**Proposed source use (NOT approved site wording):** High-priority bilingual evidence map explicitly documenting mixed outcomes. Keep source pending full-text language-aware audit.

### C04 — Frattaroli (2006), Experimental Disclosure and Its Moderators: A Meta-Analysis

**Original publisher/DOI URL:** https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.823

**Domain:** Journal writing / meta-analysis

**Evidence access:** Peer-reviewed Psychological Bulletin meta-analysis via PubMed abstract/ERIC journal record, full review restricted

**Design:** 146 randomized experiments of written or verbal emotional disclosure; random-effects synthesis.

**Findings:** Small statistically significant mean association across outcomes r=.075; moderating conditions examined.

**Limits and potentially disconfirming facts:** Studies span heterogeneous expressive writing, disclosure tasks and outcomes, not NVC observation fields. A small pooled effect is not proof of benefit in a particular individual; effect-size selection, outcomes and publication bias need complete text.

**Proposed source use (NOT approved site wording):** Add as qualified positive signal for reflective disclosure, not proof that AllNeeds journaling treats illness.

### C09 — Kim, Johnson, Ahn & Knobe (2017), The Effect of Abstract Versus Concrete Framing on Judgments of Biological and Psychological Bases of Behavior

**Original publisher/DOI URL:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-017-0056-5

**Domain:** Observation / framing experiments

**Evidence access:** Full open Springer original, including five experiments

**Design:** Five vignette-based experiments comparing abstract descriptions of psychological phenomena/behaviors with similarly matched concrete descriptions tied to an individual.

**Findings:** Abstract framing increased some judgments of biological causes and reduced some inferences of psychological origins relative to concrete cases. Context extended to non-clinical behaviors.

**Limits and potentially disconfirming facts:** Concrete descriptions can *increase* attribution of intentional or psychological causes in some tasks. Therefore the source does NOT prove concrete wording removes inference, blame or cognitive bias. Does not evaluate NVC observation-writing.

**Proposed source use (NOT approved site wording):** Particularly useful to reject over-simple 'concrete = unbiased' claims and explain that wording frames judgment.

### C10 — Moscatelli, Prati & Rubini (2019), If You Criticize Us, Do It in Concrete Terms

## Source-to-claim conclusions and explicit non-claims

1. **Emotion naming can interact negatively with another technique.** C01 (2021) and C02 (2026) show decreased benefit of reappraisal after naming in image-based tasks. This does not translate to "Feelings are dangerous" or dictate a fixed product design; it **does** rule out a universal promise that naming always reduces distress or improves subsequent reframing.
2. **Affect labeling is context- and outcome-dependent.** C03's bilingual systematic review of 43 studies is particularly useful: physiological and subjective results diverge. Retain original Korean title, DOI and language-access caveat.
3. **Emotional disclosure evidence is NOT uniformly favorable.** C04 (146 trials) estimated a small average r=.075; C05 (61 trials) found no clear benefits for objective physical health and most other outcomes. Because outcome selection and period differ, reviewing the full syntheses is necessary before comparing effect magnitudes directly.
4. **Need-support interventions and naming Needs are not synonyms.** C06/07 intervene on social contexts and autonomy-supportive practice; they are not trials of a person moving from Feeling to a named NVC Need. This is a conceptual bridge, not proven mechanism.
5. **Strategy suggestions and action plans are not synonyms.** C08's if-then planning evidence applies only when a person forms actionable context-contingent intentions.
6. **Concrete speech does not eliminate inference.** C09 found some concrete frames increase perceived psychological or intentional explanations; C10 found effects in out-group criticism but not universally in-group contexts.
7. **Emotion vocabulary and precision are not yet treatment evidence.** C11/12 are largely correlational scoping reviews, and C13 (submitted preprint) stresses inconsistent emotion differentiation→regulation associations.
8. **No direct NVC component dismantling trial was found in the searches performed.** No claim that a specified step, order, or combination has been rigorously established is authorized.
