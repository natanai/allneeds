## P01: Chen, Coccaro and Jacobson (2012)

Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ab.21407

Title: *Hostile Attributional Bias, Negative Emotional Responding, and Aggression in Adults: Moderating Effects of Gender and Impulsivity*. *Aggressive Behavior*, 38, 47–63.

The publisher abstract reports multilevel analyses of 2,749 adult twins aged 20–55. Attribution scores were associated with aggression measures, with moderation by impulsivity and gender. This is an association study, not an intervention on writing. Full statistical tables were not independently reviewed in this pass. The issue citation is 2012; the publisher's online-date field says 2014, so that field must not silently replace the bibliographic year.

**Recommendation: EXCLUDE from the public Observation guide.** It does not demonstrate a benefit from revising an observation. Using it beside wording flags could imply that an account of another person's hostility identifies a bias or an aggressive disposition. The paper remains in the research record; its exclusion is about this application, not a finding that the scholarship is invalid.

## P02: IRIS Center, Descriptive Assessments

Source: https://iris.peabody.vanderbilt.edu/module/fba-elem/cresource/q2/p06/

This official educator resource explains recording antecedent, specified behavior and consequence in the natural educational setting, including time and location. It is a methods resource, not an experimental clinical study. The official content was inspected earlier in this session; renewed retrieval returned an access error, which is recorded rather than described as a new full-page examination.

**Recommendation: RETAIN for the ABC-method paragraph only.** The preview also uses it to support frequency, duration and latency. Those are separate measurement concepts and are not established by this cited page. The proposal removes that citation stretch and makes no claim that an ABC note alone proves function, causation or intent. Recheck ordinary-browser reachability before implementation.

## P03: Koole, Schlinkert, Maldei and Baumann (2019)

Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jopy.12380

Title: *Becoming who you are: An integrative review of self-determination theory and personality systems interactions theory*. *Journal of Personality*, 87, 15–36. Online publication was in 2018; the journal issue is 2019.

The full publisher article compares two motivational frameworks and reviews their convergence and differences. It is an integrative theoretical review, not a systematic intervention-effect synthesis. Its discussion of autonomy, competence and relatedness can orient a reader to the constructs used within Self-Determination Theory.

**Recommendation: RETAIN as optional theory background.** M16 addresses the basic-needs construct more directly and is the selected core source. Neither review validates the site's wider Need vocabulary or demonstrates that naming a Need produces a clinical benefit. P03 adds no additional treatment-effect claim to the proposed guide.

## P04: Office of the Surgeon General (2023)

Source: https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf

Title: *Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation: The U.S. Surgeon General's Advisory on the Healing Effects of Social Connection and Community*.

The official advisory synthesizes research on social connection and health. Its evidence includes associations and discussion of causal and intervention evidence. It should not be dismissed as merely opinion, nor described as a trial of a communication checklist.

**Recommendation: EXCLUDE from the public Observation guide.** The evidence is relevant to social connection itself. It does not test whether writing an observation improves a relationship or health. Preserve it as a possible resource for a separately authorized Connection audit, rather than borrowing its authority for this page.
