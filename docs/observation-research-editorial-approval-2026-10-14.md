## Required corrections incorporated before approval

1. Replace the exposure study's “physiological benefit” with its measured physiological-response result. Do not equate reduced skin conductance with subjective relief or a general treatment benefit.
2. Report the spider experiment's self-reported-fear finding as no statistically significant group difference. A nonsignificant result does not prove equivalence or that nobody felt better.
3. State that concreteness training reduced rumination and overgeneralization relative to relaxation after treatment, while superiority on depressive symptoms over relaxation was not demonstrated. Briefly explain those terms.
4. Describe the emotion-naming experiments as sequence-dependent effects in unpleasant-image tasks, with immediate reported-emotion outcomes. Identify the 2026 paper as two experiments with a one-to-two-day follow-up.
5. Keep the NVC medical-student result tied to selected self-rating questions and the 153 matched assessments, rather than implying objective clinical skill or patient-care benefits. Replace the vague “small improvement” with the observed direction and distinguish the cognitive-task findings.
6. Explain appraisal and the communication-wording manipulation plainly. Keep imagined ratings separate from outcomes of real conflict conversations.
7. Explain the three Self-Determination Theory constructs while keeping them distinct from the site's broader NVC-informed Need vocabulary.
8. Remove the classification of harm or coercion as an interpretation. Explicitly preserve that information when adding event details.
9. Identify the writing examples as fictional. Counts and timestamps illustrate detail; they are not reconstructed evidence or required precision.
10. Replace Watkins's displayed PubMed record with the independently verified Cambridge publisher article landing page. Preserve functional publisher parameters and omit DOI redirects or tracking parameters from displayed URLs.

## Clinical and ethical editorial checks

The practice text adds optional detail without asking a reader to prove an account, fabricate precision, suppress feelings or reinterpret coercion. The examples preserve impact and uncertainty. Reports of harm stay intact; communication findings do not impose mutual perspective-taking or reconciliation. No instruction asks a reader to conduct exposure therapy, disclose trauma, contact another person or change treatment.

The Observation → Feeling → Need/value → Strategy pathway is labeled as the site's framework. Feelings are possibilities to explore, not deterministic evidence of a Need. Needs remain normal and valid, with apparent conflicts located in strategies or circumstances. Reflection can happen privately without another person's cooperation. Quick Check is described as local pattern matching, with no authority to assess truth, intent or credibility, and readers can ignore it.

The inference chain is checked explicitly: methods for recording events, experimental effects of wording, effects of structured therapy/training and the combination of those ideas in an app are different evidentiary questions. Findings about the first three do not establish the fourth. Mixed and nonsignificant results stay visible beside favorable findings. Clinical populations, training doses and measured endpoints remain attached to their results.
