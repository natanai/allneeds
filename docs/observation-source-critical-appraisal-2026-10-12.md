# Observation source critical appraisal — Round 2

Date: 2026-10-12. Documentation-only review on site-test. All sources are PENDING USER APPROVAL, and draft claims are NOT approved for public use. This review is targeted critical reading, not a systematic review, formal risk-of-bias scoring, or external academic peer review.

Canonical direct DOI links below have no ChatGPT referrers or tracking parameters. Access level records which text was inspected. All ten sources are also in the Round 1 approval queue.

## N01

Original: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-024-10753-2

Access: Full publisher article

Methods and actual results: Scoping review. Six databases, March-August 2023; 53 database records plus 2 additional; 16 duplicates removed; 39 texts assessed; 7 included. Two experiments, 2 reflections, one review, one mixed-methods study, one experience report. 185 empirical study participants reported.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Maps an emerging literature but cannot establish general causal efficacy. Highly heterogeneous designs, nonexperimental reports, English/Spanish/Portuguese selection; some author conclusions about conflict prevention or managerial effectiveness exceed the limited experimental base.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): A 2024 healthcare scoping review identified seven NVC publications, of which only two were experimental, and called for stronger intervention research.

Editorial assessment: Retain as evidence-map; do not use as proof of clinical effectiveness.

User decision: PENDING.

## N02

Original: https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.615e.c507

Access: Full original article (ijme.net) and PMC text

Methods and actual results: 312 of 390 students volunteered and were randomly allocated: NVC n=123, control instruction in cardiology/neurology n=189. Five half-days NVC. Only 153 had both pre and three-month follow-up (NVC 66, control 87). Only the five-item Jefferson physician empathy SUBSCORE showed adjusted 0.95 point benefit [95% CI 0.17 to 1.73]; Bayes factor for Jefferson interaction approximately 2.83. Other tests and empathy questionnaire did not significantly improve.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Approximately half the allocated group lacks matched baseline-and-follow-up data. Volunteer selection, unblinded training, short-form empathy subset, multiple outcomes; self-rating not observed patient care. Non-significant outcomes should not be construed as proof of equivalence.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): Among medical students, short NVC training modestly improved one five-item self-rated empathy score, while other measured empathy outcomes did not show clear improvements; only 153 of 312 students completed both assessments.

Editorial assessment: Retain, but require disclosure of attrition, specific scale and null results.

User decision: PENDING.

## N03

Original: https://doi.org/10.1177/00221678251412476

Access: Publisher abstract and article metadata; full paper restricted

Methods and actual results: 2026 cross-sectional study with 53 beginners and 30 experts. Some self-reported personality, fatigue, sleep and subgroup differences; not corresponding group differences on certain performance-based social-cognition tasks. Study explicitly not preregistered.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Expert and beginner groups are self-selected and likely confounded; multiple exploratory measures; no randomization. Author's causal language about NVC creating long-term benefits exceeds study design. Full effect tables not reviewed.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): A nonrandomized exploratory comparison reported some self-rated differences between experienced and new NVC practitioners, but not several performance-task differences; causal effects cannot be determined.

Editorial assessment: Hold precise claim until full article; include null results in literature map.

User decision: PENDING.

## N04

Original: https://doi.org/10.1177/10497315221128595

Access: Publisher abstract; full paper restricted

Methods and actual results: 2023 issue, first online 2022. A seven-item NVC behavior self-report measure was evaluated in a convenience sample of 205 young adults. Alpha 0.789-0.810; test-retest ICC 0.781; initial one-factor interpretation; associations with self-report empathy/emotion beliefs.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Internal consistency is not observed behavioral validity or efficacy. Convenience sample and shared self-report method; one-factor report conflicts with later three-factor model and needs reconciliation.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): Researchers developed and preliminarily tested a brief self-report measure of NVC-related behaviors; it has not validated the AllNeeds app or any treatment effect.

Editorial assessment: Retain in methods/psychometrics queue, subject to full text.

User decision: PENDING.

## N05

Original: https://doi.org/10.1177/10497315231221969

Access: Publisher abstract; full paper restricted

Methods and actual results: 2025 journal issue, online 2023. Two language samples, Chinese-speaking n=412 and English-speaking n=283; a three-factor self-connection, authentic-expression and empathic-listening NVCBS structure with measurement invariance, and negative correlation with post-traumatic stress.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Self-reported association cannot establish preventing or treating symptoms. Examine item translations, structure, effect sizes, confounding and sample characteristics before any stronger statement. Contrasts with N04.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): A cross-cultural follow-up of an NVC behavior questionnaire investigated three proposed factors and invariance in two language samples, not a clinical intervention.

Editorial assessment: Retain for full-text scale-validation review; no claims about trauma treatment.

User decision: PENDING.

## N06

Original: https://doi.org/10.1037/ocp0000058

Access: Indexed published abstract and author-posted manuscript (NOT copy of record)

Methods and actual results: German public-health organization. Nonrandom three-day training, 21 hours including practice; original baseline questionnaires 43 trained and 55 controls; paired three-month questionnaires 29 trained and 27 controls, so 56 of 98 baseline responses completed (42.9% attrition). Education years mean 15.9 trained vs 12.3 controls, p<.01. Observed discussion was only trainees, initially n=35, later n=30. Greater observed negative emotion expression, changes in self-rated NVC skills and empathic distress. Authors state workplace stressors did NOT decrease overall, but an increase was prevented. No conclusive trait-empathy benefit. Exploratory emotion-language predictor of stressors based on just 21 trained participants.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Nonrandomized, non-equivalent groups, high attrition, no active discussion comparator, artificial discussion task, unclear behavioral generalization. Author manuscript can differ from copy of record. Claims of causal workplace stress prevention are too strong.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): A small nonrandomized NVC workplace study reported increases in observed emotion language and certain self-reported outcomes; attrition, group differences and limited controls constrain causal interpretation.

Editorial assessment: Retain with strong caveats; do not claim proven conflict prevention.

User decision: PENDING.

## M05

Original: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291711002480

Access: Full peer-reviewed article through PMC

Methods and actual results: Three-arm randomized 121-person trial in major depression: primary care as usual, usual care plus guided concreteness exercises, or usual care plus structurally matched relaxation. Concreteness vs usual care HAMD improvement 4.28 (95% CI 1.29-7.26), but vs relaxation 1.98 (95% CI -1.14 to 5.11), not significant. Attrition at 8 weeks 14.9%, three months 26.4%, six months 28.1%. Guided practice included sensory imagery, unfolding sequences and next actions, not only describing an event.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Specific to major depression and guided repeated training; no NVC or quick observation prompt tested. A null difference from relaxation is NOT evidence of equivalence; study was not powered for active comparison.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): In a randomized depression trial, guided concreteness training improved symptoms compared with usual care, but its symptom advantage over matched relaxation was not established.

Editorial assessment: Retain for adjacent cognitive-training research only, not AllNeeds efficacy.

User decision: PENDING.

## M08

Original: https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612443830

Access: Full peer-reviewed manuscript through PMC; official APS abstract

Methods and actual results: 88 spider-fearful people allocated to affect labeling, reappraisal, distraction or exposure only. At one-week exposure to different spider, affect labeling lowered skin conductance versus others; marginal behavioral approach advantage versus distraction only; no between-group difference in reported fear.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Stimulus-specific small study, short follow-up; exposure and labels confounded with other program contexts; no NVC sequence. Physiological response is not equivalent to reduction in conscious fear.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): A small spider-exposure experiment found an affect-labeling difference in physiological fear responding, but not a between-group improvement in self-reported fear.

Editorial assessment: Retain under affect labeling, including null subjective outcome.

User decision: PENDING.

## M13

Original: https://doi.org/10.1002/jaba.961

Access: Full open publisher text

Methods and actual results: 596 articles from two applied-behavior-analysis journals published 2017-2021. In relevant papers with human observers of behavior, 96.4% reported interobserver agreement; 54.7% of applicable papers reported procedural fidelity and, among those reporting it, only 17.7% documented interobserver agreement on fidelity. Denominators differ from the total article count.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Publication-reporting survey, two journals and five years; agreement is not automatically truth, reliability or validity; cannot be applied to personal accounts or an unvalidated app.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): In two behavioral research journals, authors reported agreement on measured behavior much more frequently than they reported agreement on whether research procedures were implemented as intended.

Editorial assessment: Retain in scientific measurement context, not therapeutic outcomes.

User decision: PENDING.

## M14

Original: https://doi.org/10.1177/01454455221098151

Access: Publisher abstract and PubMed systematic-review metadata; full text restricted

Methods and actual results: Systematic review of six criterion-referenced ABA assessments. Found 46 studies, 37 with reliability analyses, 43 with validity; evidence conflicted and measurement methods and terminology varied.

Risk, bias, and applicability: Review does not concern NVC or AllNeeds. Need access to full paper for search methods, extraction, risk-of-bias and strength of claims; not basis for general ABA dismissal.

Tentative claim for later user consideration (not approved): A systematic review of six behavior-analytic assessments reported mixed reliability and validity evidence, showing that structured measures require dedicated validation.

Editorial assessment: Retain in methods queue; hold detailed statistical characterization until full text.

User decision: PENDING.

## Findings that correct the earlier approval queue

- N02: 312 is allocated, NOT paired follow-up. 153 have both measurements; 66 trained, 87 controls. The positive JSPE result is a five-item subset, not the full scale; the Bayesian evidence was modest. There were multiple null/unclear other empathy measures.
- N06: baseline questionnaires n=43 and 55, but paired follow-up n=29 and 27; groups differed in education. Observed verbalization tested the trained group only. Researchers found stressor increase prevented, NOT an overall decrease.
- M13: 96.4% is the denominator of articles in which a human observer measured dependent behavior, NOT 96.4% of every one of the 596 reviewed publications.
- M05: Differences versus active relaxation are non-significant; do not characterize as proof of special efficacy or equivalence. The intervention is much longer and more intensive than an AllNeeds observation.
- M08: physiological fear response diverged from self-reported fear. Both outcomes must be reported.
- N01: the conclusion about real-world NVC usefulness rests partly on nonexperimental and reflective papers.
- N03, N04, N05 and M14: reviewed against journal abstracts/records only; obtain full articles before approving detailed claims or effects.

## Continuing methods

For every proposed scholarly source, extract exact study population, randomization, comparator, pre-registration, sample flow, missing data and imputation, measure definition and changes from the full questionnaire, effect sizes and 95% intervals, null findings, risk of bias, and directness to the NVC-informed tool. No one study establishes validity of the AllNeeds Observation editor, Quick Check, or Observation→Feeling→Need→Strategy path.
