## Individual source defenses, findings and limitations

### N01 — Adriani et al. 2024 — NVC in healthcare: scoping review

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-024-10753-2  
**Research:** Seven included papers, only two experimental; other designs included reflections, review, report and mixed methods. Authors report favorable interpersonal experiences.  
**Why it belongs in review:** Map direct NVC evidence and its scarcity.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Seven heterogeneous papers cannot establish whole-process or clinical efficacy.  
**Editorial priority:** High. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N02 — Epinat-Duclos et al. 2021 — NVC education and empathy in medical students

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.615e.c507  
**Research:** 312 students randomly assigned (123 training, 189 control); 2.5-day training; self-rated physician empathy benefit +0.95 points (95% CI 0.17–1.73) at three months, not matching benefit across objective cognitive tasks.  
**Why it belongs in review:** Rare randomized NVC experiment; distinguishes self-report from objective tasks.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Small scale difference, students, no proof of clinician behavior or benefit from the app.  
**Editorial priority:** High. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N03 — Foncelle et al. 2026 — NVC expertise and social cognition

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1177/00221678251412476  
**Research:** Exploratory cross-sectional comparison: 53 beginners and 30 experts. Some personality, fatigue and sleep differences; no evidence of group differences on some perspective-taking and emotion-recognition tasks. Not preregistered.  
**Why it belongs in review:** Essential recent counterweight, including null results.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Differences between pre-existing groups do not show NVC caused any effect.  
**Editorial priority:** High. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N04 — Cheung et al. 2023 — Validation of Nonviolent Communication Behaviors Scale

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1177/10497315221128595  
**Research:** N=205 online young adults; seven-item self-report scale, alpha .789–.810, test-retest ICC .781, initial one-factor model.  
**Why it belongs in review:** Shows some measurable self-reported behaviors and enables more direct NVC research.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Neither behavioral verification nor validity of AllNeeds; later factor model differs (N05).  
**Editorial priority:** High. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N05 — Fung et al. 2025 — Cross-cultural NVC Behaviors Scale validity

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1177/10497315231221969  
**Research:** Chinese N=412 and English-speaking N=283; three-factor model (self-connection, authentic expression, empathic listening), cross-language measurement invariance; negative association with posttraumatic stress.  
**Why it belongs in review:** A serious psychometric follow-up and important comparison with N04's one-factor report.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Association with symptoms does not imply preventing or treating trauma; scale ≠ AllNeeds.  
**Editorial priority:** High. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N06 — Wacker & Dziobek 2018 — NVC workplace training

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1037/ocp0000058  
**Research:** Three-day NVC course, pre/post and three-month assessments, no-training comparator, some behavior observation; authors report higher emotion verbalization, lower empathic distress and reduced workplace stressors.  
**Why it belongs in review:** Directly relevant to workplace interpersonal communication and actual speech behavior.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Field pre/post comparison, not a matched active-controlled efficacy trial.  
**Editorial priority:** High. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N07 — Museux et al. 2016 — NVC and interprofessional teamwork

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1080/00981389.2016.1164270  
**Research:** Mixed methods with two teams, total N=9, observed collaboration tools and focus groups; some role clarification/shared planning improved.  
**Why it belongs in review:** Demonstrates feasibility of observing team collaboration.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Too small for general clinical claims; not a robust randomized design.  
**Editorial priority:** Supplemental. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N08 — Kim & Kim 2022 — NVC anger management for psychiatric inpatients

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apnu.2022.07.004  
**Research:** Non-equivalent comparison groups and six sessions; significant anger-expression/suppression differences, but not aggression or self-esteem. Abstract says 44 people while group counts 21+24=45.  
**Why it belongs in review:** Direct patient study with distinct positive/null outcomes.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Participant-count discrepancy needs full-text resolution; no general treatment claim.  
**Editorial priority:** Hold. **User decision: PENDING.**

**Why it belongs in review:** Clinical-setting feasibility and measured skills.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Self-efficacy ≠ verified abstinence or prevention of relapse; population and design limits.  
**Editorial priority:** Supplemental. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N12 — Zandkarimi et al. 2019 — NVC training and stress/anxiety/depression

**Original publisher article page:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10560-018-0584-y  
**Research:** Springer’s original abstract specifies 50 participants aged 11–18, randomly allocated 25/25 from charity centers in Tehran, eight sessions of NVC training, a no-treatment control, DASS-21 outcomes, and one-month follow-up. It reports significant reductions in **stress and anxiety, but NOT depression**. The detailed statistical analysis is still behind access restrictions.  
**Why it belongs in review:** A randomized study in a distinct adolescent population, with an explicitly null depression outcome.  
**What it does NOT justify:** Treating adolescents as adults, attributing improvement to NVC-specific components rather than attention/group support, inferring antidepressant efficacy or claiming precise effects without full methods.  
**Editorial priority:** Hold. **User decision: PENDING.**

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-018-0857-x  
**Research:** Two laboratory recall/recognition studies of concrete/abstract words under sentence contexts.  
**Why it belongs in review:** Precisely supports word-concreteness effects on certain memory tasks.

**Why it belongs in review:** Original source for concrete thinking as an investigable psychological intervention.

### M05 — Watkins et al. 2012 — Guided concreteness training, 3-arm RCT

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291711002480  
**Research:** N=121 depressed participants. Vs usual care HAMD mean difference 4.28, 95% CI 1.29–7.26; vs active relaxation 1.98, 95% CI −1.14–5.11, NOT significant.  
**Why it belongs in review:** Counterbalanced randomized evidence for concrete-processing exercises.  
**What it does NOT justify:** No proof of advantage over active relaxation or effectiveness of allneeds Observation.  
**Editorial priority:** High. **User decision: PENDING.**

**Why it belongs in review:** Relevant to the Feeling step and potential emotional-language mechanisms.

**Editorial priority:** High. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N13 — Yang & Kim 2016 — Integrative review of NVC intervention studies

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.16952/pns.2016.13.1.36
**Research:** Five Korean databases, 2005-2015; 12 studies, of which nine were quasi-experimental, alongside one each qualitative, mixed and case-report design. Authors called for stronger trials and noted multi-session group training.
**Why it belongs in review:** Finds otherwise missed non-English literature and provides evidence-map context that complicates reliance on the later 2024 healthcare review.
**What it does NOT justify:** Counts are not additive to the 2024 review, or proof of intervention efficacy.
**Editorial priority:** High. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N14 — Nosek, Gifford & Kober 2014 — NVC education in nursing students

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.5430/jnep.v4n10p1
**Research:** Single-group pre/post mixed methods; IRI empathy score changed from mean 69.1 to 71.4, p=.037, plus qualitative diaries and focus groups.
**Why it belongs in review:** Original training study and a caution about what an uncontrolled quantitative change establishes.
**What it does NOT justify:** No untreated comparison, no demonstration NVC caused the change, and two-year qualitative follow-up is not measured long-term quantitative benefit.
**Editorial priority:** Supplemental. **User decision: PENDING.**

### N15 — Jiang et al. 2026 — NVC randomized educational trial

**Original:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09621-w
**Research:** 198 fourth-year students randomized 100 vs 98; five 2-hour sessions; no missing outcomes. Full 20-item self-rated empathy improved relative to a time-matched standard course by 16.95 points (95% CI 15.40–18.50, g=3.02); a study-shortened eight-item observer-scored role-play communication checklist improved by 4.44 points (95% CI 3.41–5.47, g=1.19). Raters masked to group allocation but not pre/post.
**Why it belongs in review:** Direct NVC-trained communication performance and active time-matched comparator — a rare advance over prior studies.
**What it does NOT justify:** No preregistration, only short-term one-center simulated outcomes, unusually large empathy effect requiring replication, control lacked equally intensive deliberate-practice/feedback method, study-specific abbreviated observation checklist, no real clinical outcome or isolated Observation effect.
**Editorial priority:** High, pending complete supplementary material and risk-of-bias audit. **User decision: PENDING.**

## Material checks and conflicts to preserve

- N02 randomized NVC training: reported self-rated empathy advantage of 0.95 points at three months; do not inflate to clinical behavioral improvement. Objective tasks did not establish equivalent changes.
- N03 observational beginner/expert differences cannot establish causality; retain its measured null effects.
- N04 vs N05: one-factor pilot self-report scale versus later three-factor cross-cultural measurement model; review details, not paper over disagreement.
- N08: the PubMed abstract reports total sample 44 but 21+24=45 in listed arms. Do not give confident total before reading full participant flow.
- M05: active-controlled randomized concreteness training was not statistically different from relaxation on depressive symptom outcomes, despite differences from treatment as usual.
- M08: physiological fear measure improved while self-reported fear did not differ between study groups.
