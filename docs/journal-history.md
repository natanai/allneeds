# Journal and emotional history

Approved for implementation on `site-test` on 2026-10-11. Start from current `main`; keep the change on the review branch until phone and desktop review is complete.

## Ownership and continuity

`JournalPage` owns route opening, local repository updates, backup/restore, and the shared full-screen shell. `JournalBook` owns writing, read-only pages, page navigation, and independent composer drafts. `HistoryWorkspace` owns graph settings and review controls. `HistoryChart` draws local SVG and exposes keyboard-operable points; it requires no external chart service. `historyModel` owns filtering, local calendar windows, aggregation, denominators, summaries, and CSV serialization.

Saved entries remain schema 1 under `allneeds.v2.journal`. Compatible optional fields are:

- `mood`: `{ kind: 'rated', value: -5..5, arousal?: -5..5 }`, `{ kind: 'mixed' }`, or `{ kind: 'unsure' }`. `value` remains the compatible valence/pleasantness coordinate; new point placements include both coordinates. Omission means not rated, and omission of only `arousal` identifies a valid older valence-only page.
- `experiencedAtISO`: the optional time the recorded moment happened. Charts and page ordering use this when present; otherwise they use `dateISO`.
- `createdAtISO`: the original creation time for new entries. Existing `dateISO` remains unchanged by editing and remains the creation-time fallback for older entries.
- Feeling `ratingSource: 'unknown'`: a legacy feeling label had no recorded number. Its former display fallback remains compatible, but history never uses it as a reported intensity.

Previously saved entries that already contain an explicit numeric fallback without provenance cannot be distinguished from actually entered ratings. Do not infer unknown provenance from the number 5 alone or rewrite users' historical ratings. Newly migrated unnumbered labels retain unknown provenance through subsequent saves.

The existing new-entry draft key is retained. Edit drafts use `allneeds.v2.journal.draft.<entryId>`, which is already recognized by the profile reconciliation draft-preservation policy. A draft left by the older combined composer is moved to its edit key once. Reading pages does not write a draft. New and edit drafts flush on page changes, page hide, visibility change, and unmount. Saving or clearing one draft does not clear another.

Annotations use `allneeds.v2.journal.annotations`, a versioned local repository. Generic browser backups and explicitly saved profile snapshots carry this key. It follows the existing settings-source choice during profile reconciliation, rather than silently merging conflicting annotations. Graph view settings are session-local, under `allneeds:history-view:2`, and are not included in browser/profile backups. The version change establishes Pleasantness and Arousal as the default without carrying the former intensity default through an already-open tab. Graph controls then survive Journal opening/closing, route visits, and a reload in the same tab.

Normal account merging, browser backup, restore, and repository normalization preserve the optional entry fields. No backend schema or account-authentication change is required. There is no automatic sharing, diagnosis, text-based emotion inference, or telemetry of personal content.

## Plot and review rules

The initial window is today and the preceding 29 local calendar days. Other presets are 7 days, 90 days, all time, and inclusive custom dates. All time extends from the first recorded moment through the latest moment or today. Windows end exclusively at the next local midnight; navigation changes calendar days rather than assuming every day is 24 hours. Dates on the plot are proportional to elapsed milliseconds.

Guided Journal records also preserve the user's selected body clues (including user-selected intensity), their original overall-feeling dimensions, and explicit body-clear/no-word-yet choices as optional structured check-in context. A canonical Feeling explicitly rated 1–10 in the candidate sheet becomes a genuine `JournalFeelingRating` with that intensity; choosing `Fits` without a rating preserves `ratingSource: 'unknown'`. Choosing `Maybe` or `Not this time` clears a prior numerical rating for that word. Working/other emotion words and Faux Feelings remain separate guided-support roles rather than silently becoming canonical Journal Feeling ratings. These fields remain inspectable with the original reflection and are searchable in History; they are not promoted to invented valence/arousal values or unasked feeling intensity. Newly saved check-ins carry `source: 'lane'` for provenance.

The Journal and the Overall feeling sheet in Alexithymia Support use the same valence–arousal picker. The whole plane changes color with the user-selected position, using the current Customizer roles. Support-lane pleasantness/energy coordinates (0–1 for matching) convert to the Journal's −5..5 valence/arousal on handoff. Only a pair of selected coordinates creates a numeric mood; Mixed, Not sure and missing ratings stay nonnumeric. Optional power and expectedness details still feed support-lane matching but are not renamed as valence/arousal.

The default mood view contains two lines on one fixed −5 to +5 axis: Pleasantness (`mood.value`) and Arousal (`mood.arousal`). A legacy page without arousal contributes only Pleasantness. Intensity has a fixed 0 to 10 axis, with selected feelings rated 1 to 10. Frequency has a fixed 0 to 100 percent axis. Mixed, Not sure, missing coordinates, unselected feelings, and unknown intensity never become numeric zero. Frequency zero means no matching logged entry mentioned the selected feeling, not that the feeling was absent.

Multi-day views automatically group records by local calendar day; an elapsed viewport of one day or less shows individual timestamped records on an hourly axis for mood and intensity, even when its start and end straddle midnight. Touch horizontal drag pans through time; two-pointer pinch scales using the full two-dimensional finger distance, so vertical and diagonal pinches also work; desktop trackpad horizontal wheel pans and Ctrl/trackpad pinch zooms. Only the chart cancels Safari's native pinch gestures; one-finger vertical page scrolling is preserved. The visible window and period recap update without rounding gesture timestamps to calendar-day boundaries. Preset date selection and Reset zoom restore the calendar-aligned view. Keep fixed vertical numeric axes, user-authored ratings, and native vertical scrolling; suppress clicks after real drags. A date-attached label (for example, `Oct 13 · 2 entries`) reports total matching entry count when screen space allows; its connector extends from the displaced label to the exact source-day plot position so mobile edge clamping cannot visually reassign it to another date. When the experienced date differs from the saved date, day-list entries display the original saved date for clarity. The underlying aggregation still uses the experienced moment when explicitly recorded. point accessible names always retain this count. Tapping a daily point, badge, or unrated-day marker opens that day with its complete matching entry list. Back restores the original period while retaining filter changes. Each series draws a straight line through its recorded points in chronological order, including across dates without a rating. The line is a visual connection between observations, not an estimated value for the time between them. Aggregates use the arithmetic mean of reported values only, retain min/max, rating count and total entry count, and link to every matching page in that day. In the overview, plot mean points alone instead of unlabelled vertical range whiskers. Show the recorded range in a readable, compact line when someone opens a day and at least two reported values for that measure differ. Screen-reader descriptions of aggregated points also retain the reported range and number of ratings; printing uses the same uncluttered plot. A mean does not represent all of the individual moments. Missing valence/arousal ratings are excluded independently from their means, never imputed as zero. Frequency uses mention count over all matching entries in a day, including entries with no selected feelings. There is no smoothing or inferred daily score.

Filters apply before grouping and before recap calculations. A selected feeling in the filter means the entry mentions that feeling; selecting a series chooses what to plot. Intensity thresholds require at least one reported selected-series rating in the interval, or any reported feeling when viewing mood. The unfiltered intensity interval does not discard entries without feeling ratings.

The mood recap reports separate medians, ranges, and rating counts for Pleasantness and Arousal. Other numeric views use the first plotted series' raw reported ratings, not medians of daily averages. Entry and day counts include all matching entries. Frequency uses the matching-entry denominator. These are descriptions of recorded moments, not estimates for unrecorded time.

Comparison uses the immediately preceding window with the same number of calendar days and identical filters. It aligns earlier marks by day within the window. Tooltips and journal pages retain the real earlier dates. Different logging frequency or selected contexts may affect a comparison.

Coincident timestamps remain separate records. Hit targets expose nearby points together when their marks overlap, so a page cannot be hidden behind another mark. The secondary entry log remains an alternative route to every matching page, including unscored pages.

Feeling, Need, and Tag metadata share one quiet divider-led form section. Feeling and Need selectors use the standard site sheet instead of anchored popovers. Their stable title, instructions, scrollable choices, and Clear/Done actions remain visible and usable on small screens; opening a selector does not summon the mobile keyboard until its search field is chosen.

CSV exports the current matching entries with settings and provenance; reflections require opt-in. User text is quoted and spreadsheet formula prefixes are neutralized. The printable recap includes the graph, period, counts, selected settings, comparison, visible context notes, and optional reflections. Files are created locally only on an explicit export action.

## Phone and desktop review

1. Open Journal. Confirm the restored full-screen composer opens on the blank, unruled newest page. On phones the reflection sheet should dominate the opening viewport and the original secondary controls should follow below it; on desktop they should occupy the original right sidebar.
2. Open Emotional coordinates in the full-screen Journal. Drag through multiple quadrants, confirm the point color changes with the active Customizer palette, and confirm the pleasantness/valence and arousal numbers update beneath it. Try Mixed and Not sure independently of the numeric center.
3. Use the minimal page-turner at the bottom. On the blank page only a left arrow should appear when saved pages exist. On the oldest page only a right arrow should appear. No disabled arrow, New entry button, page count, or date-jump control should appear. Return all the way right and check that the unfinished blank-page draft is unchanged.
4. Edit a saved page, leave it without saving, then return and edit again. Check that this edit and the new-page draft survive independently after reload.
5. Save a page with an earlier experienced date. Confirm its page order and graph point use that date while its original save time remains available.
6. On History, confirm the edge-to-edge phone graph precedes its controls and defaults to separate Pleasantness and Arousal daily-mean lines. Pan horizontally and pinch zoom in/out; verify the dates, plotted samples and period recap follow the viewport, and that normal vertical swipes still scroll the page. Drag without accidentally opening a journal page, then use Reset zoom. Verify a dated label such as `Oct 13 · 2 entries` is readable and opens the intended day. Repeat horizontal wheel/trackpad zoom on desktop. Then select 7/30/90 days, all time, and custom dates. Navigate periods and zoom. Tap a day with multiple entries, a count badge, and an entirely unscored day: each opens a single day, its individual graph, and the complete page list. Open a page and return; tap Back to restore the prior range and filters. Confirm coincident individual timestamps remain accessible.
7. Compare two or three feelings, daily/weekly averages, and frequency. Inspect counts and ranges. Filter by a tag or Need and compare with the previous period.
8. Close Journal from a graph point. The measure, period, zoom, and filters should still be there.
9. Add a context note. Print/export first with reflections off, then with reflections on. Verify only the intended period's entries are included and that CSV has separate Valence and Arousal columns.
10. Try every Customizer preset, narrow portrait width, large text, keyboard-only controls, and reduced motion. Long entries should remain readable with vertical scrolling.
