# allneeds psychological model

> Status: canonical product-model contract for Needs, Feelings, Observations, and Strategies. Read this before auditing, writing, implementing, or redesigning psychological content.

allneeds uses an NVC-informed psychological model in which people move from situations and observations toward feelings, underlying Needs or values, and possible Strategies for tending those Needs. Research is used to examine and support the model carefully. The model itself is a product/framework commitment and must not be presented as though every part of it were a settled scientific taxonomy.

This document defines the psychological target that every Need page and every Need audit must preserve.

## Core model

The site's practical pathway is:

**Observation / situation → Feeling → Need / value / motivation → Strategy**

These are different kinds of information.

- **Observations and situations** describe what happened or what a person is responding to. They are a doorway into exploration, not the final explanation of experience.
- **Feelings** are lived experiences that can help a person notice that something matters. They are clues for exploring Needs, not deterministic proof that one exact Need or cause is present.
- **Needs** are treated by the site as basic human values or motivations: things such as safety, connection, honesty, autonomy, understanding, rest, or support that can matter across many different situations.
- **Strategies** are concrete ways people try to tend Needs. A person, action, outcome, demand, or particular solution is a Strategy rather than the Need itself.

A single situation can involve many Feelings and many Needs. A Feeling can be associated with several possible Needs. The site should support exploration rather than pretending that emotional inference is certain or one-to-one.

## Each Need has a working definition

Every audited Need must have a concise **working definition** that states what the site means by that Need, value, or motivation.

The definition is part of the site's psychological model and belongs in the short Evidence copy. It should usually take about one sentence or one compact clause. It is not the central thrust of the page. Its purpose is to keep the conceptual object stable as more Needs are audited so readers, editors, researchers, and future agents can tell what the site is and is not referring to by a particular label.

A good working definition should:

- describe the Need in ordinary language rather than as a circular dictionary entry;
- be broad enough to cover the lived range the site intends to include;
- be narrow enough to distinguish the Need from neighboring concepts and particular Strategies;
- describe the value or motivational concern itself, not one preferred way of satisfying it;
- avoid implying that the site's framework label is a formally established scientific construct unless the evidence actually supports that claim;
- remain compatible with the strongest evidence used on the page.

The definition may be an editorial/framework synthesis. It does not need to masquerade as a quotation or a scientific consensus definition. Research should inform and constrain it, and the Details section should make clear what empirical and theoretical evidence lies underneath the broader page.

The definition should be present early enough in the short Evidence copy that a reader can orient to what the page means, but it should not crowd out the page's larger job of explaining why the motivation can make sense, what function it may serve, and what someone can do with it.

This requirement supersedes older guidance that said not to define a Need at all. The prohibition was intended to prevent overclaiming formal scientific definitions, not to leave the site's own concepts undefined.

## Needs do not compete or conflict

This is a standing allneeds model commitment.

**Needs themselves do not compete with or conflict with one another.** Apparent conflicts occur at the level of Strategies, circumstances, limited resources, timing, available information, available support, or the range of solutions people can currently imagine.

Examples of language to avoid:

- `Honesty conflicts with Privacy.`
- `Autonomy must be balanced against Connection.`
- `Safety competes with Freedom.`
- `Sometimes one Need has to win over another.`

Those framings turn Needs into opposing forces and violate the site's model.

Instead, describe the actual Strategy-level problem when it matters. A person may not yet know how to speak honestly while protecting information they want kept private. Two people may prefer different actions while both caring about autonomy and connection. Limited time or resources may prevent every Need from being tended as fully as someone would like in a particular moment. None of those constraints make one Need the enemy of another.

The site's aspirational direction is to look for Strategies that can include as many Needs as possible, including the Needs of other people when they are relevant. Not finding such a Strategy immediately does not make any Need invalid. Real constraints and losses can remain, and grief can be an appropriate response when an all-needs Strategy is not available.

Validating a Need does **not** validate every behavior or Strategy used in its name. Harmful, coercive, unsafe, or otherwise inappropriate Strategies remain open to ethical, practical, legal, and clinical evaluation. The Need underneath them can still be treated as intelligible and worthy of attention.

## Every Need is normal and valid

A Need page should never make the reader feel that the presence of the Need is itself a defect to correct.

The site treats every listed Need as a normal human value or motivation that can make sense to experience. The intensity, timing, interpretation, or Strategy associated with a Need may require reflection. The existence of the Need does not require justification before it is allowed to matter.

Public copy should therefore avoid language that subtly pathologizes a Need, such as treating it as excessive, immature, selfish, irrational, weak, dangerous, or something a healthier person would no longer experience.

The desired reader response is approximately:

> “Oh. It makes sense that this matters to me. Other humans experience this too. There are understandable reasons this motivation exists, and it may be useful information about what matters to me. I can do something to tend to it.”

The site does not need to say those sentences literally. The page should create that understanding through accurate, well-chosen evidence and humane writing rather than reassurance pasted on top of weak claims.

## What a Need page is for

A person often arrives at a Need page because something in their life has already activated that Need. They may be ruminating about a conversation, relationship, decision, threat, disappointment, uncertainty, or unmet expectation.

The page should help them move one level deeper than the triggering situation.

A strong Need page should help the reader:

1. **Orient to what the Need means here.** Give the reader a concise working definition so the page's use of the label is clear.
2. **Recognize the motivation.** The page should make the Need recognizable as a real human concern rather than an abstract dictionary term.
3. **Understand why it can make sense.** Explain credible psychological, developmental, social, evolutionary, anthropological, clinical, or other evidence that helps make the motivation intelligible and potentially functional.
4. **Reduce shame or self-rejection without sentimental reassurance.** The evidence should make it easier to see the Need as worth listening to rather than something that must be suppressed merely because it is uncomfortable or inconvenient.
5. **Separate the Need from one triggering situation or one preferred solution.** The reader should be able to recognize that the Need can potentially be tended in more than one way, even if the original person or circumstance does not change.
6. **Restore practical agency.** Strategies should give the reader concrete things they can try so they can shift from repeatedly thinking about the problem toward tending the underlying Need.

Need pages are therefore not neutral encyclopedia entries. They are evidence-grounded psychological resources designed to support recognition, understanding, self-compassion, and movement toward workable action.

## Evidence should serve the model without overstating science

Research is not included merely to prove that a Need label has academic prestige. It should help the reader understand the motivation and its possible function.

During a Need audit, actively look for evidence addressing questions such as:

- Do people reliably show motivation toward this value or condition even when doing so has a cost?
- What recurrent human problem might this motivation help people navigate?
- What social, developmental, cognitive, physiological, or relational functions are associated with it?
- Are there evolutionary, anthropological, behavioral-ecology, or cultural-evolutionary accounts that help explain why humans may care about it?
- What does modern psychological or clinical research show about how the motivation operates now?

Evolutionary grounding is valuable when the scholarship genuinely bears on the Need. It does not have to be omitted merely because the evolutionary account remains debated. When appropriate, state the epistemic status directly with language such as `some scholars propose`, `evolutionary accounts suggest`, or `one hypothesis is`. Do not turn a plausible account into a claim that a discrete evolved Need module has been demonstrated.

Likewise, do not claim that a site Need is an experimentally established `basic psychological need` unless the cited literature actually supports that classification. The site's use of **Need** is its framework vocabulary. Empirical claims inside that framework still require precise support.

Boundary distinctions can be useful when they prevent a real conceptual error, but they should not dominate a page merely to demonstrate taxonomic completeness. Do not manufacture conflict with neighboring Needs in order to explain a distinction. For example, a Honesty page does not need to defend Privacy as `compatible with Honesty`; the model already allows both Needs to be present together.

## The short Evidence copy has a therapeutic job

The short Evidence section is often the first substantial text a reader sees after identifying with a Need. It should include the Need's concise working definition, then quickly move into why the motivation may be present and why its presence can be useful or understandable.

The definition is an orientation point, not the thesis of the whole description. Avoid turning the short copy into a dictionary entry followed by caveats. The reader should come away knowing both what the site means by the Need and why that Need may make sense to experience.

The short copy should provide enough scholarly grounding that the reader can feel the page is taking their experience seriously. Important conceptual distinctions belong there only when they are necessary to understand the Need or when a public citation depends on that idea. Details can carry the deeper research explanation.

Do not let source coverage turn the short copy into a compressed literature review.

## Details should deepen recognition, not replace it with an audit report

Details remains source-first and claim-calibrated. Explain what researchers studied, what they found or proposed, and how that helps illuminate the Need.

The narrative should still preserve the reader-facing purpose of the page. Research about the Need should answer some version of `Why might humans care about this?`, `What can this motivation help us do or notice?`, or `How does it operate?`

Do not make the Details section primarily a catalogue of what the Need is not. Do not use repeated caveats to create distance from the reader's experience. Narrow claims accurately from the start, then explain the evidence in ordinary language.

## Strategies must help tend the Need

The Strategies deck is the practical continuation of the page's psychological model.

A strategy should help the reader do something with the Need they have recognized. It should not merely give them a new way to keep analyzing the triggering situation.

System strategies should therefore:

- have a direct, intelligible pathway to tending the audited Need;
- be concrete enough to try without requiring the reader to solve the entire original problem first;
- preserve choice and avoid moral pressure, compulsory disclosure, confrontation, or dependence on another person's cooperation when a self-contained version is possible;
- remain low risk and clinically defensible under `docs/system-strategy-evidence-standard.md`;
- help widen the reader's available Strategies rather than implying one person, outcome, or behavior is the only way the Need can be met;
- avoid framing another Need as something that must be defeated or suppressed for the strategy to work;
- earn their place through a distinct useful pathway rather than deck-size symmetry or filler.

Preparation, practice, reflection, environmental changes, requests, connection with resources, or other actions can all qualify when they genuinely help tend the Need and meet the evidence standard.

The deck as a whole should make it easier for someone to move from `I am stuck in the situation that brought this up` toward `Here are some things I can try for the Need itself.`

## Audit check before approval

Before presenting any full Need audit proposal, explicitly verify internally that:

1. The short Evidence copy contains a concise working definition that makes clear what the site means by this Need without claiming a stronger scientific definition than the evidence allows.
2. The page treats the Need as normal and valid rather than as a problem to eliminate.
3. The opening helps a reader understand why the motivation can make sense and may serve a useful function.
4. Research grounding is strong enough to support that understanding, with contested theories labeled as theories rather than omitted or overstated.
5. The page does not frame Needs as competing, conflicting, or requiring one Need to defeat another.
6. Any boundary distinctions clarify concepts without turning neighboring Needs into adversaries.
7. The Need remains distinct from particular people, demands, outcomes, or Strategies.
8. The page gives the reader a path from recognition toward agency.
9. Every proposed system strategy directly tends the Need, is clinically defensible, and can be tried without first resolving the triggering situation whenever possible.
10. The strategy deck contains no filler kept merely for symmetry, count, or inherited legacy status.
11. The final copy still meets the separate evidence, citation, proposal-format, and human-editorial-style rules.

If a proposal fails this model check, it is not approval-ready even if its individual citations are technically accurate.
