# Observation held-studies appraisal — Round 5 (2026-10-13)

**Branch:** natanai/allneeds:site-test documentation-only. **Scope:** all ten prior HOLD cases. This is an academic/clinical-style claim-relevance and methodological appraisal, **not** a review conducted by a licensed clinician or an independent systematic review, formal GRADE evaluation or final user approval.

**Editorial outcomes for the ten:** 3 promoted narrowly to RETAIN (N13, M04, M11); 4 remain HOLD (N03, N08, N09, N12); 3 EXCLUDED from the public Observation guide (M17, C10, C13). All ten still have **user decision PENDING**, and all proposed source-to-claim language remains unpublished.

**New overall proposals across the 48 existing candidate sources:** 32 RETAIN, 4 HOLD, 12 EXCLUDE. These are *provisional editorial decisions*. A RETAIN does not mean the paper is proven true in every respect or its proposed wording approved.

## Method of continued scrutiny

Revisited original journal publisher pages and indexed PubMed records for N03/N08/N09/N12/M04/M11/M17/C10/C13. Read the **complete** original 12-page Korean/English N13 article via university institutional provenance and readable journal-copy PDF; reviewed methods/tables/selection flow. Read the **complete** ten-page M04 accepted/published article copy, including active comparator, participant flow, intention-to-treat assumption and null/problem-solving outcomes. For six others restricted access to full effect tables is explicit. Searched separately for publisher and academic repository copies and conflicting/null findings. **No source is a direct AllNeeds app trial.**

**Citation-link policy:** Publisher's article page or original journal scholarly archive is the display URL, with no DOI redirect, tracking or ChatGPT-generated referral. DOI remains bibliography metadata only. Extra author/institution PDF access links are given separately, clearly labeled.

## Source-by-source decisions

### N03 — Foncelle et al. (2026) — NVC expertise and social functioning

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00221678251412476

**Evidence access:** Publisher complete abstract, preregistration disclosure, study record and linked data/materials repository; restricted full article; raw participant data and supplementary analyses not yet independently assessed.

**Design and sample:** Exploratory comparison of 53 novice and 30 expert NVC practitioners, Bayesian comparisons across a broad battery of cognition, dispositional and self-rated life-quality measures. Investigators explicitly state no preregistration.

**Findings including null results:** Experienced participants reported differences in some traits/fatigue/sleep measures, including a male-specific subgroup; no convincing differences for certain performance-based visuospatial perspective-taking or facial affect categorization tasks.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Cross-sectional, self-selected groups, many possible confounders and subgroup tests, no allocation or measured training dose, vulnerable to reverse causation. Authors' abstract moves from association to suggestions of long-term benefit, not justified by design. Full methods and effect tables restricted.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** Direct NVC evidence discussion only, especially null task measures and what cross-sectional evidence cannot prove; never clinical outcomes or causality.

**Editorial judgment: HOLD.** Methodological causal problem cannot be solved by full-text alone; full effect tables/data would permit accurate descriptive synthesis. Not an efficacy citation. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**


### N08 — Kim & Kim (2022) — NVC-based anger course in psychiatric ward

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0883941722000772

**Evidence access:** Full publisher article landing page with introductory text, highlights and abstract; numerical results tables/full Methods not provided.

**Design and sample:** Nonequivalent pretest/posttest groups in a psychiatric inpatient unit; six individual one-hour sessions; abstract states total n=44 and arms n=21 versus n=24 (45).

**Findings including null results:** Statistically significant between-group results on anger expression and suppression, but not aggression or self-esteem. Publisher highlights' recommendation of accessible treatment exceeds demonstrated outcomes.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Number-inclusion discrepancy blocks confidently stating statistical sample. No random allocation, active comparator, patient diagnosis/distribution and dropout unknown. Reported aggression nonsignificance is especially pertinent clinically.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** Direct clinical NVC intervention research only, not Observation component; abstain from treatment recommendations.

**Editorial judgment: HOLD.** Need published full participant flow, group characteristics and complete between-group effects. No claims regarding reduced aggressive acts. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**


### N09 — Yang & Kim (2021) — NVC communication program in inpatient alcohol care

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ppc.12673

**Evidence access:** Wiley version-of-record abstract and bibliography; 'full' link resolves to abstract, no full Methods or statistical tables in accessible record.

**Design and sample:** Mixed-methods, quasi-experimental training among 47 patients in two Korean psychiatric hospitals.

**Findings including null results:** Publisher abstract states significant changes in measured empathy, anger expression, communication skill and abstinence self-efficacy, p<.001.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Unclear between-group vs pre/post calculations, exposure, follow-up, attrition, and reliability of instruments. Two hospital comparison may confound intervention with hospital practice. The authors' aim of relapse prevention is NOT observed abstinence or relapse outcome.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** Direct NVC clinical training history only; not addiction treatment efficacy or AllNeeds journal claim.

**Editorial judgment: HOLD.** Need group-level results and outcome timing; separate self-efficacy from objective abstinence or relapse. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**


### N12 — Zandkarimi, Kamelifar & Heshmati-Molaee (2019) — NVC and adolescent distress

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10560-018-0584-y

**Evidence access:** Original Springer landing page/abstract and indexed paper preview. Detailed full Methods/results tables restricted.

**Design and sample:** Fifty economically vulnerable girls ages 11–18 in Tehran, randomly allocated 25/25 to eight-session group course or no-treatment control; Depression Anxiety Stress Scales-21, pre/post and one-month follow-up.

**Findings including null results:** Abstract reports decreases in stress and anxiety but NOT depression; prior title shorthand 'young women' should not imply adult sample.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Small specific adolescent sample, no equivalent social-contact treatment, short follow-up; allocation concealment, baseline comparability, effect sizes, adverse effects and multiple outcomes not fully accessible. Do not infer clinical treatment for depression.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** NVC clinical research library only, with crucial null depression result and population label.

**Editorial judgment: HOLD.** Still need authorized full text to score risk of bias, attrition and outcome magnitudes. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**


### N13 — Yang & Kim (2016) — integrative review of Korean NVC interventions

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://kiss.kstudy.com/Detail/Ar?key=3429800

**Additional readable full-text provenance:** Source academic university archive (PDF provenance): https://s-space.snu.ac.kr/bitstream/10371/96364/1/13-1-36-%EA%B9%80%EC%84%B1%EC%9E%AC-%EC%B5%9C%EC%A2%85.pdf ; readable 12-page copy hosted at https://scispace.com/pdf/integrative-review-of-nonviolent-communication-intervention-3bfbcs5hgt.pdf

**Evidence access:** Original complete 12-page article with Korean text, bilingual abstract and English Tables 1–3, flowchart; journal index cross-checked against publisher-supplied academic KISS record and Seoul National University archive provenance.

**Design and sample:** Five Korean academic databases/search sources, journals Jan 2005–Dec 2015, NVC communication intervention eligible. 120 screened initial records; 69 excluded title/abstract, 18 duplicates, 33 assessed as full text, 21 subsequently excluded, 12 studies included. Of 12, nine quasi-experimental, one qualitative, one mixed methods, one case study; eleven group interventions.

**Findings including null results:** Tables show highly varied populations and outcome questionnaires and delivery doses: students, couples, families, employees; NVC programs often multi-session (not the app's short Observation prompt). No pooled standardized effect or causal isolation of four NVC elements.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Only Korean papers, 2005–2015, unpublished theses excluded, overlap with other global reviews unquantified. Authors' quality check was a study-characteristics matrix judged all included papers appropriate, not independent validated RoB 2/ROBINS-I scoring. Korean narrative discussion not independently linguistically validated by a specialist; English tables and abstract are accessible and verifiable.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** Explicit historical 'state of NVC evidence' section; qualifying study counts and design with exact period/selection. This is the strongest correction to possible English-language selection bias.

**Editorial judgment: RETAIN.** Now verified against complete article. Retain strictly for evidence-map claims, not evidence of efficacy; further assess individual Korean primary studies for any clinical outcomes. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**


### M04 — Watkins, Baeyens & Read (2009) — concreteness cognitive-bias training

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037%2Fa0013642

**Additional readable full-text provenance:** Accessible paper copy checked for full Methods/Results: https://cdn2.psychologytoday.com/assets/attachments/84564/concretenesstrainingproofofprinciplejap2009.pdf

**Evidence access:** Full original journal article copy (10 pages), PubMed bibliographic publisher record. Not to be confused with Watkins & Moberly (2009) Behavioral Research and Therapy pilot, DOI 10.1016/j.brat.2008.10.014.

**Design and sample:** Three randomized conditions: repeated guided concreteness-thinking exercises, bogus training matched rationale/experimenter attention/time, and waiting-list. Original 97 screened; 10 missed posttraining visit (6 active, 3 matched, 1 waitlist), leaving 60 who completed (20/20/20). One initial 1.5–2h researcher session and 30-min daily training for seven days.

**Findings including null results:** Depression scales and concrete processing improved vs waiting-list and matched training; rumination improved versus waitlist. Authors report that one-time measures of problem-solving solution count/effectiveness did not improve. Their conservative intent-to-treat sensitivity carried forward baseline scores for missing outcomes, an assumption, not observed data.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Uneven dropout across conditions; dropouts had higher initial clinician-rated depression; 1 week follow-up only, no sustained outcome or actual change in depression diagnosis; participants dysphoric (not universal general population); small groups and partly self-rated endpoints. Later stronger 2012 trial found no difference in depression outcomes vs structurally matched relaxation.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** Optional background/related-experimental-method evidence on *repeated guided concrete cognitive processing*, not the Observation editor's instructions or NVC as therapy.

**Editorial judgment: RETAIN.** Full text supports a specific controlled finding with good matched-rationale comparator. Keep explicit protocol and attrition and later inconclusive active-comparator contrast. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**


### M11 — Sanford (2006) — appraisals and observed marital communication

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037%2F0893-3200.20.2.256

**Evidence access:** PubMed indexed abstract plus original publisher-preview reproduction; full statistical estimates and ancillary tables not independently audited.

**Design and sample:** 77 recently married heterosexual couples, four conflict discussions over two sessions; appraisals before conversation; observed communication ratings analyzed with hierarchical/multilevel models.

**Findings including null results:** Within-person shifts in partner-understanding expectations, negative-communication expectations and attributions predicted shifts in actual conversational behaviors. Findings strongest for expected understanding; associations remained after controlling for expectation accuracy.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Repeated real behaviors add ecological relevance vs hypothetical vignette studies. However appraisals were neither experimentally manipulated nor randomly assigned, so a person's/partner's behavior may change both appraisals and outcomes; restricted couple/relationship context; no evidence editing an Observation leads to conflict benefit.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** Communication/interpretation section as supporting *association between appraisals and conduct*, alongside M09 controlled hypothetical evaluations. Never claim causal mechanism.

**Editorial judgment: RETAIN.** It specifically tests appraisals before subsequently observed discussions, adding value not fully duplicated by hypothetical ratings; use one concise causal-agnostic sentence. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**


### M17 — Tang, Wang & Guerrien (2020) — SDT needs and older-adult wellbeing

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pchj.293

**Evidence access:** Original Wiley peer-reviewed abstract/PubMed meta-analysis record; full tables unavailable in this pass.

**Design and sample:** Systematic review of 23 studies, including 17 in meta-analysis, linking basic-need satisfaction, autonomous motivation and wellbeing among older adults, primarily Western populations; literature search also Chinese databases.

**Findings including null results:** Positive associations with favorable wellbeing and negative associations with depressive/apathy measures, across the studied older-adult samples; intervention or experimentally manipulated Need labeling not reported.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Population limited, largely correlational; wellbeing outcomes and SDT constructs do not assess AllNeeds 'Need' definitions; overlaps M16/ C06/C07 and adds no unique Observation claim.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** Research-only Needs/aging background, potentially useful for future targeted Needs content, not current Observation guide.

**Editorial judgment: EXCLUDE.** Credible scholarship with poor direct claim fit and redundant coverage, which can deceptively appear to validate NVC needs selection. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**


### C10 — Moscatelli, Prati & Rubini (2019) — concrete versus abstract criticism

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0261927X19864686

**Evidence access:** SAGE publisher abstract and Bologna university repository record; full analysis behind access restrictions.

**Design and sample:** Two group-criticism experiments, n=76 and n=77, comparing in-group and out-group criticism and specific/abstract descriptions.

**Findings including null results:** Concrete out-group criticism elicited less negative response than abstract out-group criticism; in-group criticism was not similarly affected in the first experiment. Second study found lower inferred hostility/prejudice by an out-group critic when criticism was concrete.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Group identity and critique of social groups are essential moderators; not the same setting as writing one's personal observation to examine feelings; without effect/tables, no more generalized inference is warranted.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** Group communication social-psychology appendix only, not central first-person Observation research.

**Editorial judgment: EXCLUDE.** Evidence credible for niche intergroup feedback; too indirect and likely to be misleading as a broad NVC interpersonal benefit claim. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**


### C13 — Whiting & Nook — emotion differentiation and regulation systematic review

**Publisher / original journal record:** https://lemolab.princeton.edu/publications/systematic-review-research-emotion-differentiation-and-emotion-regulation

**Evidence access:** Original Princeton author laboratory full abstract and submission status; author manuscript preprint mentioned but full details/independent review not completed.

**Design and sample:** Submitted (not peer-reviewed) systematic review of 31 empirical studies examining associations between emotional differentiation and emotion regulation.

**Findings including null results:** Research literature is inconsistent: some associations suggest beneficial regulation, others null or in opposite direction; measurement heterogeneity likely matters.

**Risk-of-bias, generalizability and inference constraints:** Scientific concern is substantive and important, but review is submitted, not externally peer-reviewed or fully methodologically audited. A 31-study review can identify uncertainty but is not a trial or proof that labeling increases/decreases symptoms.

**Fit to AllNeeds guide:** Internal contradictory-evidence watch, not public clinical authority. Contradictory findings should inform conservative public wording drawn from peer-reviewed studies C01/C02/C11/C12.

**Editorial judgment: EXCLUDE.** Exclude from approved public citations at its current submitted/unverified status; preserve for monitoring future journal publication and checking original empirical papers. **User decision: PENDING. No public integration authorized.**

## Notable corrections and new evidence

1. **N13 can be promoted from HOLD to RETAIN as historical evidence mapping**, as complete original article was obtained. Selection flow: 120 initial; 33 full text; 12 included; 9 quasi-experimental and 11 groups. It is not a meta-analysis and does not prove NVC efficacy. The journal article's title, dates and bilingual tables confirm correct provenance. A complete specialist Korean-language bias assessment and trial duplication check remain outside this pass.
2. **M04 is DISTINCT from Watkins & Moberly (2009).** M04 is Watkins, Baeyens & Read (2009), APA Journal of Abnormal Psychology, controlled bogus-training and wait-list. Watkins & Moberly's same-year, similar-title study used adjunctive relaxation comparison and is a separate paper. Do not splice methods or outcomes from them. M04's completed N=60 came after TEN nonattenders, concentrated in active training n=6 vs n=3 and n=1; nonattenders were more severely depressed by clinician rating. Formal ITT used baseline-carried-forward values.
3. **M11 has genuine observed interaction, not hypothetical ratings**—more naturalistic than M09, though nonrandom and causally ambiguous.
4. **C10's key moderator is group membership:** specifically out-group criticism and concrete feedback; a generic claim 'specific wording reduces conflict' would misrepresent the study.
5. **M17 is a legitimate systematic review but not direct NVC evidence.** Exclusion from this guide should not be confused with criticizing SDT or older-adult research.
6. **C13 remains a submitted review.** The negative/null study evidence merits monitoring and cross-checking primary research, but submitted status does not justify citing it as a settled peer-reviewed conclusion.
7. **N08/N09/N12 are not approved treatment-efficacy evidence**, even though publisher abstracts report selected favorable associations. Null outcomes, comparator problems and full data-access limits remain.
8. **N03 performance-task null findings must stay visible**, and any statements claiming long-term NVC expertise *causes* enhanced quality of life remain prohibited.
